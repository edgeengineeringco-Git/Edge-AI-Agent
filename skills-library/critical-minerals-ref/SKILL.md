## 2. Lithium

### Occurrence patterns

**Pegmatite-hosted (spodumene, lepidolite, petalite):**
- LCT (Li-Cs-Ta) pegmatite family
- Associated elements: Cs, Rb, Ta, Nb, Be, Sn, B (tourmaline)
- Spodumene: Li₂O 3.7–8% theoretical; ore grade target > 0.5% Li₂O (cut-off), economic > 1.0% Li₂O
- Geochemical halo: Rb/Sr ratio > 2, Cs > 10 ppm, Ta > 20 ppm, Be > 5 ppm in surrounding rocks
- Alteration: albitization, muscovitization, greisenization in wall rocks
- Typical dimensions: 10–500 m length, 1–50 m width, tabular to irregular

**Brine deposits:**
- Salars (evaporite basins): Li > 150 mg/L (cut-off), economic > 300 mg/L
- Mg/Li ratio < 6–8 is critical (low Mg reduces processing cost)
- Key markers: low Mg, elevated B, K, Rb, Cs; high aridity index
- Li-rich basins: Atacama, Uyuni, Clayton Valley, Cauchari

**Sedimentary/clay:**
- Hectorite smectite (Clayton Valley type): > 800 ppm Li
- Mafic volcanic tuffs: > 1000 ppm Li; Thacker Pass (Rhyolite Ridge) style
- Geochemical association: Mg, B, F; near-alkaline lacustrine setting

### Analytical notes

- pXRF sensitivity for Li is essentially zero (Li Z=3, below typical detection range)
- Li must be determined by ICP-MS/OES after lithium-specific digestion or by LIBS (laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy)
- Proxy indicators via pXRF: Rb > 200 ppm, Cs > 5 ppm, Ta > 10 ppm, Nb > 50 ppm suggest LCT pegmatite potential

## 3. Cobalt

### Occurrence patterns

**Sediment-hosted (SEDEX/stratiform):**
- Cu-Co Copperbelt (DRC/Zambia): Co 0.1–0.5% with Cu 1–5%
- Host: siliciclastic-carbonate sediments, reduced zones
- Pathfinders: Cu, Co, Ni, Mn, V; elevated organic carbon

**Magmatic sulfide (Ni-Co-Cu):**
- Norilsk, Sudbury, Voisey's Bay type
- Co typically 0.02–0.15% alongside Ni 0.3–2%, Cu 0.5–3%
- Pentlandite [(Fe,Ni)₉S₈] and cobaltite [(Co,Fe)AsS] as primary carriers
- Pathfinders: Ni, Cu, Pt, Pd, Te, As, S; mafic-ultramafic hosts

**Laterite (saprolite-hosted):**
- Developed on Ni-Co peridotite/serpentinite
- Co 0.08–0.15% (cut-off ~0.08%), Ni 0.5–1.5%
- Erythrite [Co₃(AsO₄)₂·8H₂O] as gossan indicator (vivid pink)
- Deep weathering profile required (> 20 m saprolite)

**Hydrothermal vein (5-element Ag-Ni-Co-As-Bi):**
- Cobalt camp (Ontario) style: Co 0.3–5%, Ag, Ni, As, Bi
- Native silver, smaltite, cobaltite in vein
- Economic at high grade (> 0.5% Co) but small tonnage

### Economic thresholds

| Type | Cut-off | Economic Grade |
|---|---|---|
| SEDEX Cu-Co | 0.08% Co | > 0.15% Co |
| Magmatic sulfide | 0.04% Co | > 0.08% Co |
| Laterite | 0.06% Co | > 0.10% Co |

## 4. Copper

### Occurrence patterns

**Porphyry Cu (±Mo, Au):**
- World's largest Cu source; Cu 0.2–2%, Mo 0.01–0.05%, Au 0.1–0.5 g/t
- Calc-alkaline magmatic arc setting; large (> 1 km diameter) low-grade bodies
- Alteration zones (center to edge): potassic (K-feldspar, biotite) → phyllic (sericite, pyrite) → argillic (kaolinite, smectite) → propylitic (chlorite, epidote)
- Pathfinders: Mo, Re, Au, Ag, As, Sb in halos; chalcopyrite, bornite, molybdenite in ore
- Cut-off: 0.2–0.4% Cu (open pit), 0.7–1.5% (underground)

**IOCG (Iron Oxide Copper-Gold):**
- Olympic Dam type; Cu 0.3–1.5%, U₃O₈ 0.02–0.08%, Au 0.3–1.5 g/t
- Massive magnetite/hematite with chalcopyrite, bornite
- REE enrichment (0.1–0.5% TREO) common — important polymetallic context
- Pathfinders: Fe (magnetite), Cu, Au, U, LREE, Bi, Co

**VMS (Volcanogenic Massive Sulfide):**
- Zn-dominant or Cu-dominant varieties
- Cu 0.5–5%, Zn 1–15%, Pb 0.2–3%, Ag 10–200 g/t, Au 0.1–5 g/t
- Seafloor exhalative; altered footwall (chlorite, sericite)

**Sediment-hosted (Cu-Ag stratiform):**
- Kupferschiefer, Zambian Copperbelt, Udokan
- Cu 1–5%, Ag 20–150 g/t; stratiform in shale/siltstone above oxidized red-bed sequence

### pXRF Cu detection

Cu is reliably detected by pXRF down to ~10–30 ppm. For sulfide-hosted deposits, test both fresh rock face and gossan/oxide zone. Malachite [Cu₂CO₃(OH)₂] and azurite [Cu₃(CO₃)₂(OH)₂] in gossans are strong visual + pXRF indicators.

## 5. Nickel

### Occurrence patterns

**Magmatic sulfide (komatiite-hosted):**
- Kambalda type; Ni 0.5–4%, Cu 0.1–0.5%, Co 0.02–0.1%
- Pentlandite, pyrrhotite in peridotite/komatiite lava channels
- Pathfinders: Ni, Cu, Co, Cr, S; geophysical signature (EM conductor)
- High MgO (> 15%), Cr (> 1000 ppm), Ni/Cu > 5 in host rock indicate prospective lithology

**Laterite (saprolitic + limonitic):**
- Limonite zone: Ni 0.8–1.5% as goethite substitution
- Saprolitic zone: garnierite [(Ni,Mg)₃Si₂O₅(OH)₄] — Ni 1–4% (higher grade)
- Requires > 30 m weathering, tropical setting, ultramafic basement
- Si/Mg ratio decreases with depth; Ni increases toward saprolite-bedrock contact
- Cut-off: 0.8–1.0% Ni (limonite, HPAL processing), 1.0–1.5% Ni (saprolite, pyrometallurgy)

**Pentlandite in MORB/layered intrusion:**
- Sudbury, Jinchuan, Platreef types
- Ni 0.3–2%, Cu 0.3–1.5%, PGE 0.5–10 g/t
- Disseminated to massive sulfide in gabbroic/noritic intrusions

### Pathfinder summary

| Type | Primary Pathfinders | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Komatiite sulfide | Ni, Cu, Co, S, Cr, MgO | PGE, Te, Se |
| Laterite | Ni, Co, Mn, Fe (goethite) | Cr, Si, Mg depth profiles |
| Pentlandite intrusion | Ni, Cu, Co, PGE, S | Bi, Te, As |

## 6. Tungsten

### Occurrence patterns

**Skarn (scheelite-dominant):**
- WO₃ 0.3–2%, with Mo, Cu, Bi, Au
- Ca-Mg skarn at granite-carbonate contact; garnet, pyroxene, epidote, wollastonite gangue
- Scheelite fluoresces bright blue-white under shortwave UV (254 nm) — definitive field indicator
- Cut-off: 0.2–0.4% WO₃ (open pit), 0.5–0.8% WO₃ (underground)
- Pathfinders: W, Mo, Bi, Cu, Ca, F (fluorite)

**Vein-hosted (wolframite/huebnerite/ferberite):**
- High-temperature quartz veins in or near granites; W 0.3–3% WO₃
- Wolframite [(Fe,Mn)WO₄] is dense (7.1–7.5 g/cm³), sub-metallic, black
- Associated with Sn (cassiterite), Mo, Bi, As
- Pathfinders: W, Sn, Mo, Bi, As, Li, Rb, Cs

**Stockwork and greisen:**
- W ± Sn in granite greisenization zones
- Muscovite, fluorite, topaz replacing K-feldspar

### pXRF for Tungsten

W K-α at 59.3 keV is detectable by higher-energy pXRF instruments (Olympus Vanta with Au tube). Detection limits typically 50–200 ppm WO₃. Scheelite concentrate assay by fusion-XRF preferred.
