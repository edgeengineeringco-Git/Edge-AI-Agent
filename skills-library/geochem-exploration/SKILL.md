# Exploration Best Practices

Pure reference — no scripts. Use this skill when planning a field campaign, reviewing sampling procedures, or deciding whether a geochemical anomaly warrants follow-up.

---

## 1. Sampling

### Rock chips
- Minimum 1–2 kg per sample
- Chip systematically across full exposure width
- Avoid oxidized surfaces unless gossan sampling is the objective

### Soil
- B-horizon (15–40 cm depth) for geochemical surveys
- > 200 g per sample
- Dry before splitting

### Stream sediment
- -80 mesh fraction for heavy minerals
- -150 mesh for dissolved load
- Collect from active channel

### Auger / RAB drilling
- Minimum 3 m composites
- Sample every interval
- Retain duplicates

---

## 2. Grid Spacing Guidelines

| Target Size | Appropriate Grid Spacing |
|---|---|
| Porphyry Cu (> 1 km) | 400 × 400 m regional; 100 × 100 m detail |
| Carbonatite body (100–500 m) | 100 × 100 m → 50 × 50 m |
| REE vein/dyke (10–100 m wide) | 50 × 50 m → 25 × 25 m |
| IAC clay zone (> 1 km) | 200 × 200 m |
| Pegmatite (10–50 m) | 25 × 25 m |

---

## 3. Quality Assurance in the Field

1. Insert a **blank** (clean quartz sand) every 20 samples
2. Insert a **certified reference material** (CRM matching expected grade) every 20 samples
3. Collect a **field duplicate** (second split from same location) for ≥ 10% of samples
4. Log sample coordinates to ± 1 m GPS accuracy
5. Photograph every sample at collection with scale bar and sample tag in frame
6. Record matrix type, moisture, oxidation state, and any visible mineralogy

---

## 4. When to Continue vs. Cease

### Continue investigation when

- Anomaly is spatially coherent across ≥ 3 adjacent samples
- Th/U > 4 with eTh > 30 ppm on gamma and coincident pXRF REE anomaly
- Grade > 2× background over ≥ 200 m
- Known deposit analogue in same geological province within 50 km

### Cease and re-evaluate when

- All anomalies are single-point, isolated, no spatial pattern
- CRM recovery consistently outside 85–115% — data cannot be trusted
- Background TREO > 500 ppm (contamination or field-wide anomaly; not prospective without contrast)
- Deposit type interpretation changes fundamentally after mapping (e.g., interpreted carbonatite turns out to be calc-silicate metasediment — reassess entirely)
