### 1.4 Redox chemistry and Ce anomalies

| Element | Stable Valence | Anomalous State | Redox Potential (V) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ce | +3 | +4 (only REE) | Ce³⁺ → Ce⁴⁺: +1.61 V |
| Eu | +3 | +2 (reducing) | Eu³⁺ → Eu²⁺: −1.93 V |
| Yb | +3 | +2 (reducing) | Yb³⁺ → Yb²⁺: −1.42 V |
| Others | +3 | (rare) | — |

**Ce anomaly interpretation:**
- Ce/Ce* > 1.2 (positive anomaly) — oxidizing conditions preserve Ce⁴⁺ (rare)
- Ce/Ce* < 0.8 (negative anomaly) — reducing conditions or seawater REE pattern
- Crustal average shows positive Ce anomaly

**Eu anomaly interpretation:**
- Eu/Eu* < 0.7 — feldspar fractionation (Eu²⁺ compatible with Ca²⁺ in plagioclase)
- Eu/Eu* > 1.3 — plagioclase cumulate or hydrothermal Eu²⁺ enrichment

## 4. Processing and Separation Technologies

| Eudialyte (alkaline) | Flotation ± gravity | 30–50 % REE | 50–70 % |

### 4.2 Leaching technologies

| Ore Type | Lixiviant | Conditions | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bastnäsite | H₂SO₄ 6 M, 90°C | 85–95 % | Roast-acid; high energy |
| Monazite | NaOH 2 M, 300°C | 85–98 % | Caustic crack |
| Monazite | HCl 6 M, 90°C | 90–98 % | Alternative to caustic crack |
| Eudialyte | HCl 6 M, 70°C | 60–75 % | Silica gel formation risk |

| IAC clay | NH₄Cl 0.5 M, 90–100°C | 60–85 % | Heap or in-situ |

### 4.3 Separation and purification

| Method | Selectivity | Recovery | Cost | Applications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ion exchange | Excellent | 95–99 % | Medium–High | Magnet-grade Nd, Pr |
| Solvent extraction (SX) | Excellent | 98–99 % | Low–Medium | Most industrial-scale |
| Precipitation (selective) | Good | 85–95 % | Low | Bulk HREE separation |
| Liquid–liquid extraction | Excellent | 95–98 % | Very High | Ultra-pure specialty REE |
| Electrolysis (molten salt) | Pure single elements | 90–95 % | Very High | Nd, Dy, Tb metal production |

**Magnet-grade separation cost:** 20–35 % of total REE processing cost. Projects requiring separated Nd/Pr are only economic at high grades (> 2 % TREO) or with integrated processing.

### 4.4 Refining to oxide and metal

## 7. Environmental and Health Considerations

### 7.1 Radioactivity in REE deposits

| Source | Half-life | Typical Activity | Health Risk | Processing Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Th (in monazite) | 1.4e10 yr | 100–500 Bq/g in concentrate | High (α, γ) | Radiation worker protocols |
| U (in some REE) | 7e8 yr (U-238) | 10–100 Bq/g | Moderate (α) | Less problematic than Th |
| La, Ce, Pr, Nd | Stable | — | None | — |

**Regulatory classification:** Monazite concentrate > 200 Bq/g = radiation source material (license required). Ore < 200 Bq/g = NORM (exemption thresholds vary).

**Mitigation:** Caustic crack (wet) generates Th-bearing residue requiring radioactive waste disposal. Dry roast + acid leach can separate Th as insoluble residue (easier to manage). Ion exchange + SX produces Th-depleted final products (~99.99 % removal).

### 7.2 Environmental footprint by processing route

| Process | Acid Intensity | Tailings (t/t REE) | Toxic by-product | Recycle Potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bastnäsite (H₂SO₄) | Very High | 10–15 | Acid mine drainage | Partial (>90 %) |
| Monazite (caustic) | Medium | 5–8 | Th-rich residue | Full REE (Th separate) |
| Monazite (acid) | Medium–High | 6–10 | Th + U residue | Moderate (85–90 %) |
| Eudialyte (HCl) | High | 8–12 | SiO₂ gel (clogging) | Challenging (70–75 %) |
| IAC (in-situ leach) | Low | <1 | Ammonium leachate | Excellent (>95 %) |

**Carbon intensity (t CO₂/t REE):** Calcination: 0.5–1.5 · Leaching + SX: 0.3–0.8 · Electrolytic metal: 2–5 · In-situ clay leach: 0.1–0.3 (lowest).
