6. Policy, Regulation and Strategic Context

BREAKING — Sweden Grants Norra Kärr 25-Year Mining Lease (28 June 2026)

Swedish Government Approves Norra Kärr Heavy REE Exploitation Concession: On 28 June 2026, the Swedish government granted a 25-year mining lease for the Norra Kärr heavy rare earth elements project in Jönköping County, owned by Leading Edge Materials Corp. (TSXV: LEM). The deposit is one of Europe's richest HREE sources, with high proportions of dysprosium, terbium, and yttrium. Energy and Industry Minister Ebba Busch stated the deposit could satisfy a large portion of Sweden's and the EU's supply needs. All government agencies and the Mining Inspectorate had recommended approval. The company now proceeds to environmental permitting, updated pre-feasibility study, and offtake/financing discussions. This is the most significant European REE mining approval in decades and strongly validates the policy narrative supporting non-Chinese CRM exploration investment.

  • Jurisdiction: Sweden / European Union
  • URL: SVT — Regeringen ger gruva i Norra Kärr grönt ljus

G7 Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance (Évian, 17–19 June 2026)

G7 Targets Sub-60% Single-Supplier Dependency by 2030: At the 2026 G7 Summit in Évian, leaders announced a dedicated Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance. Targets reducing reliance on any single supplier of rare earths and permanent magnets to below 60% by 2030 (ambition: 50%). The IEA will provide market monitoring and early warning. Traceability pilots start with lithium and nickel, expanding to five new minerals annually. Since early 2026, 195 critical minerals projects have been announced totalling €64 billion ($74 billion) in investment. Canada announced multiple bilateral deals including Ucore–Sumitomo rare earth separation facility in Ontario and Torngat Metals–Schneider Electric collaboration on the Strange Lake REE project.

  • Jurisdiction: G7 (Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, UK, US, EU)
  • URL: PM Canada — G7 Leaders' Declaration on Critical Minerals

European Union — CRMA Implementation and RESourceEU Action Plan

60 Strategic Projects Selected Under CRMA: The EU has approved 60 Strategic Projects benefiting from streamlined permitting and facilitated access to finance. The RESourceEU Action Plan (adopted December 2025) operationalises the CRMA through €3 billion in de-risked and blended finance, coordinated stockpiling, joint purchasing mechanisms, and export restrictions on waste and scrap streams. A new European Critical Raw Materials Centre is to be proposed in 2026. CRMA amendments are being negotiated, with the Council adopting its general approach in March 2026 and Parliament preparing its position. Final agreement expected in 2027. Member States must adopt penalty regimes for CRMA non-compliance by 24 November 2026.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: NPELitigation — CRMA Executive Compliance Guide

Ireland — CRMA National Measures and Exploration Activity

Ireland's CRMA Obligations Advancing: Ireland's Whole-of-Government Circular Economy Strategy 2026–2028 includes targets for CRMA compliance: national measures on circularity for CRMs (report due 24 May 2026 and annually thereafter), measures to promote recovery of CRMs from extractive waste (by 24 November 2027), and identification of products containing permanent magnets within two years of CRMA implementing acts. Group Eleven Resources reported exceptional copper-silver-antimony drilling results at Ballywire (23.60% Cu over 0.6m, among the highest grades in Ireland in 60 years), with 67,000–75,000m drilling programme underway. Unicorn Mineral Resources identified gravity anomalies at Kilmallock along the same Rathdowney Trend. Boliden Tara Mines received planning permission for a 3 km exploration tunnel at ~1 km depth to assess the Tara Deep ore body (27 Mt inferred resource). The ISIF has committed €30M to the Irish Minerals Fund.

  • Jurisdiction: Ireland
  • URL: Group Eleven — Ballywire Drilling Results

Nordic Mining Corridor — Europe's Emerging Critical Minerals Powerhouse

Norway Fen Deposit, Sweden LKAB/REEtec Integration, Finland Sokli: The Nordic mining corridor is rapidly consolidating as Europe's primary critical minerals hub. Norway's Fen deposit (15.9 Mt TREO, Europe's largest) targets first production late 2031 with ~800 t/yr NdPr. LKAB's critical minerals industrial park in Luleå (environmental permit received) will process rare earths and phosphorus from iron ore residual streams. LKAB is largest shareholder in REEtec, developing industrial-scale separation at Herøya. Finland's Sokli project progresses under CRMA framework. The corridor benefits from abundant renewable hydropower, decades of mining expertise, transparent regulation, and proximity to European manufacturing hubs.

  • Jurisdiction: Norway, Sweden, Finland / EU/EEA
  • URL: EuroMining News — Nordic Mining Corridor

China — Rare Earth Export Controls Continue to Disrupt Markets

Ex-China HREE Prices at 100–150x Pre-Control Levels: China's April 2025 export controls on seven heavy rare earths continue to cause extraordinary price dislocation. Rotterdam yttrium prices at ~US$1,100/kg (140x pre-control levels). Dysprosium oxide at ~US$1,450/kg externally (4–5x internal Chinese price); terbium at ~US$4,500/kg (350–450% increase). China's Rare Earth Price Index stands at 262.6 (2.6x 2010 baseline). Near-zero Chinese HREE exports to Japan in May 2026. Chinese brokerages predict domestic supply tightness will persist with annual mining permit growth capped at 10%. The sustained dislocation strengthens the economic case for non-Chinese HREE exploration across all target regions.

  • Jurisdiction: Global / China
  • URL: Rare Earth Exchanges — Price Index Analysis

UK — £50M Critical Minerals Programme and Magnet Hub

UK Government Launches £50M Package (22 June 2026): The UK announced a £50M critical minerals programme including a £25M Critical Minerals Accelerator, £20M Rare Earth Magnet Hub, and £5M demand aggregation platform. The Magnet Hub competition opens first, followed by the Accelerator. This builds on £200M+ already committed. The HyProMag/Mkango Birmingham plant — the UK's first commercial rare earth magnet facility in 25 years — is already supplying recycled NdFeB magnets to Siemens. HyProMag's German subsidiary (Pforzheim) completed first commissioning runs with capacity scaling to 750 t/yr.

  • Jurisdiction: United Kingdom
  • URL: UK Parliament — Written Statement on Critical Minerals

European Rare Earth Magnet Recycling Capacity Expands

Multiple New Recycling Facilities Across Europe: June 2026 saw significant expansion in European REE recycling capacity. Italy's RarEarth received €2.5M EIC grant for its first production plant recovering rare earths from end-of-life motors. The LIFE INSPIREE project in Ceccano, Italy, is building the EU's first industrial-scale REE recycling plant (up to 2,000 t/yr of permanent magnets). Paladin Envirotech expanded into the Netherlands (Helmond) via R&L Recycling acquisition, deploying its RECapture technology. Germany now hosts three operational magnet recycling facilities: HyProMag Pforzheim (750 t/yr), Remloy Bitterfeld (500 t/yr, acquired by Mkango for €8M), and the Birmingham plant supplying Siemens. This rapidly expanding downstream ecosystem creates growing demand for domestic REE feedstocks — directly supporting the case for upstream exploration investment.

  • Jurisdiction: EU / UK
  • URL: StartupBusiness — RarEarth EIC Grant
