6. Policy, Regulation and Strategic Context

G7 Summit — Critical Minerals Alliance Launched (Évian, 17–19 June 2026)

G7 Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance: At the 2026 G7 Summit in Évian-les-Bains, leaders announced a dedicated Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance to reduce dependence on any single supplier of rare earths and permanent magnets to below 60% by 2030 (with an ambition of 50%). The IEA will provide market monitoring and early warning of supply disruptions. Traceability and transparency pilot mechanisms will start with lithium and nickel, expanding to five new critical minerals each year. The G7 also agreed to explore joint procurement, price-gap subsidies, quotas, and price floors to support non-Chinese supply chains. 195 critical minerals projects have been announced since the start of 2026, representing €64 billion ($74 billion) in investment.

  • Jurisdiction: G7 (Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, UK, US, EU)
  • URL: US News — G7 Critical Minerals Alliance ↗

European Union — Three-Supplier Rule Drafted

EU Drafts "Three-Supplier Rule" for Critical Supply Chains (June 2026): The European Commission is drafting a mandatory "three-supplier rule" that would require companies in sensitive sectors to source critical components and raw materials from at least three different suppliers in different countries, with a single-supplier cap of ~30–40%. The rule covers critical raw materials, chemicals, EV batteries, rare earths, and pharmaceuticals. EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič publicly announced the proposal on 5 June 2026. The rule would apply to large companies initially, with transition periods for SMEs. Discussions are expected at the EU leaders' summit. The rule faces WTO compatibility concerns from China and cost-burden criticism from industry.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Reuters — EU Three-Supplier Rule ↗

EU CRMA Penalty Regimes Deadline — 24 November 2026: Member States must adopt penalty regimes for CRMA non-compliance by 24 November 2026, with administrative fines and potential withdrawal of strategic project status as enforcement mechanisms. The European Parliament's ITRE committee vote on CRMA amendments is scheduled for 24 June 2026.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Eversheds Sutherland — CRMA Updates ↗

  • URL: UK Government — Critical Minerals Investment ↗

China — Rare Earth Export Controls Bite Harder

Yttrium Prices Remain at 100–150x Pre-Control Levels: China's April 2025 export controls on seven heavy rare earths (Sm, Gd, Tb, Dy, Lu, Sc, Y) continue to cause extraordinary price dislocation in ex-China markets. Rotterdam yttrium prices stand at ~$457.50/kg (vs ~$5.5K/tonne domestic Chinese price — a 100x+ spread). Dysprosium oxide trades at ~$1,450/kg externally (8x internal price); terbium at ~$4,500/kg (5x spread). Japan's Tosoh has suspended zirconia powder supply due to raw material shortages. Rare earth magnet exports from China fell 35% month-on-month in May 2026. Chinese brokerages (CITIC Securities, 22 June 2026) predict domestic supply tightness will persist with annual mining permit growth capped at 10%.

  • Jurisdiction: Global / China
  • URL: The Oregon Group — Rare Earth Price Surge ↗

China's New Mineral Resources Law Regulations (Effective 15 June 2026): China's revised Mineral Resources Law implementing regulations entered into force on 15 June 2026, designating 36 minerals (including REE, tungsten, lithium) as strategic resources. The regulations introduce national security reviews for foreign mining investments and fast-track strategic mineral reserve buildout, signalling even tighter state control over the critical minerals value chain.
