# EDGE Critical Minerals Intel — Weekly Brief
**Date:** 2026-05-18

## Executive Summary

- **Lithium surges ~50% YTD** past ¥200,000/tonne ($28k/t), driven by record battery output (+66% YoY) and supply constraints from Zimbabwe's ore export ban and Jiangxi production halts
- **Cobalt prices more than doubled** since early 2026 to ~$56,000/tonne, driven by DRC export restrictions that have upended supply chains for NCM cathode producers
- **Copper faces deepening structural deficit** — multiple production downgrades at Grasberg, Kamoa-Kakula, and Reko Diq push 2026 shortfall estimates as high as 529kt (Scotiabank)
- **EU and US sign landmark Critical Minerals Agreement** (April 24, 2026), enabling EU-origin minerals to count toward IRA EV tax credits and elevating CRM cooperation to national security level
- **November 10, 2026 deadline** looms for China's expanded rare earth export controls suspension — the single most consequential event on the horizon for heavy REE supply chains

## 1. Market Movements

### Lithium
- **Lithium carbonate** surged past ¥200,000/tonne (~$28,000/t), hitting a high of ¥205,260/tonne on May 13. Prices are up ~50% year-to-date.
- **May battery output** exceeded expectations: total battery output +10.5% MoM, +66% YoY; LFP battery output +16% MoM, +72% YoY.
- **Supply tightness**: Zimbabwe's full ban on lithium ore exports (Feb 2026) continues to block shipments; Jiangxi mine license renewals forcing production halts through May–June; Greenbushes (Australia) cut annual production guidance.
- **Analyst divergence**: UBS forecasts ¥298,000/tonne, CITIC targets ¥250,000/tonne in 2–3 months, while Goldman Sachs expects H1 2026 peak then easing to ¥101k–160k/t.
- **Key structural shift**: Energy storage batteries now account for ~31% of lithium demand (up from 23%), driven by AI data center and grid storage buildout.

### Rare Earth Elements
- **China's April 2025 export controls** remain in full force — heavy REEs (dysprosium, terbium, yttrium) exports down ~50% vs. pre-controls.
- **Prices outside China**: dysprosium/terbium 4–5x domestic Chinese prices; yttrium up to 140x premium. U.S. aerospace companies temporarily paused production due to yttrium shortages.
- **Expanded controls suspended**: October 2025 measures (12 elements, extraterritorial 0.1% threshold) suspended through November 10, 2026 — a key deadline.
- **New May 2026 controls**: China added export controls on rare earth **technologies** (mining, smelting, processing) and updated the "unreliable entities list" targeting U.S. defense tech companies.
- **China's REE compound exports fell 17% in value** (TrendForce, May 18) as alternative supply chains gain momentum.

### Cobalt & Nickel
- **Cobalt**: ~$56,290/tonne (~$24/lb), up ~67% YoY and more than doubled since early 2026. DRC export restrictions (Feb 2026) remain the primary driver. Cobalt sulphate (EXW China) ~$13,046/tonne.
- **Nickel**: ~$18,508/tonne, up ~19% YoY but down ~3.9% on the week (profit-taking). Nickel sulphate (EXW China) ~$4,888/tonne. Indonesia production cuts and Russian supply disruptions adding upward pressure.
- **Chemistry shift**: LFP batteries gaining share, reducing cobalt dependency; nickel-rich chemistries preferred for high-performance EVs. Battery-grade nickel demand expected to roughly triple by 2030.
- **Supply chain crisis**: Major automakers (Tesla, Ford, GM) pursuing direct mining partnerships and vertical integration.

### Copper
- **Price**: ~$13,000–$14,500/tonne (~$5.90–$6.58/lb), driven by structural deficit.
- **Deficit estimates**: ICSG ~150kt, J.P. Morgan ~330kt, Scotiabank now at ~529kt (revised up from 350kt after recent mine downgrades).
- **Major supply hits**: Grasberg Block Cave remains closed (mudslide); Kamoa-Kakula guidance slashed 24-31% to 290-330kt; Reko Diq deferred.
- **New supply pipeline**: 7 Chilean projects starting (~494kt/yr new capacity); Lundin's Vicuña (Argentina/Chile) targeting 400kt/yr Cu; Foran's McIlvenna Bay (Canada) 91% complete, mid-2026 commercial production.

### Graphite & Battery Minerals
- **China controls ~90% of global anode production** and ~98% of graphitization capacity — the critical bottleneck.
- **US anti-dumping duty of 93.5%** on Chinese anode-grade graphite (July 2025) is reshaping the market.
- **Syrah Resources**: 24kt produced Q1 2026 at Balama (Mozambique); targeting first commercial AAM sales from Vidalia, Louisiana in H2 2026. Received ~$165M in Section 48C tax credits.
- **NextSource Materials**: Signed binding agreement with Syrah for 34-68kt graphite fines over 7 years for UAE anode facility; multi-year Mitsubishi Chemical offtake.
- **Nouveau Monde Graphite** (Quebec): ~$645M financing secured, commissioning targeted end of 2028.
- **BNEF**: Technical graphite deficit globally projected by 2032; ex-China battery demand growing 14% annually.

## 2. Geopolitical Developments

### China Export Controls
- April 2025 controls on 7 medium/heavy REEs remain in full force with case-by-case licensing.
- October 2025 expanded controls (12 elements, extraterritorial) suspended through November 10, 2026 — three possible outcomes: extension, selective reinstatement, or full reimposition.
- **May 2026**: New controls on rare earth **technologies** (mining, smelting, processing IP) and expanded "unreliable entities list" targeting U.S. defense companies.
- **China's Foreign Trade Act amendment** (effective March 1, 2026) strengthens legal authority to sanction foreign governments imposing discriminatory restrictions.

### Russia's Rare Earth Ambitions
- **700 billion ruble (~$7.5B)** investment announced in April 2026 to build independent REE supply chain from mining through refining.
- Russia holds ~29 million tonnes of REE reserves (3rd globally) but lacks separation/refining capacity — historically ships raw ore to China.
- Full self-sufficiency estimated at 10+ years; China holds 60%+ of global REE patents.
- Russia positioning as alternative supplier to Europe if sanctions ease.

### U.S. Countermeasures
- **FORGE Initiative** launched (Feb 2026) at 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial with 54 countries + EU — successor to Minerals Security Partnership. Korea first rotating chair.
- **Project Vault**: $10B+ EXIM strategic stockpile with dynamic rotation mechanism.
- **Greenland**: Push for sovereign base areas and mining rights; Tanbreez deposit transferred to US-based Critical Metals.
- **Brazil, Ukraine, DRC, Australia**: Bilateral CRM agreements signed or advanced.
- **Sprott Rare Earths Ex-China ETF (NASDAQ: REXC)** launched Q1 2026.

### Central Asia & Polar Silk Road
- Kazakhstan emerging as strategic intermediary — developing tungsten and REE processing capacity.
- China's Polar Silk Road reengaging Arctic mineral resource discussions.

## 3. Policy & Regulation

### United States
- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, July 2025)**: Cancelled 30D EV tax credit but preserved industrial tax credits critical for minerals. Created Industrial Base Fund ($5B), National Defense Stockpile Transaction Fund ($2B), Critical Minerals Loan Program (up to $500M/project).
- **Defense Production Act** support grew from $209M (FY2024) → $550M (FY2025).
- **MP Materials**: $400M DoW investment + $150M loan for heavy REE separation at Mountain Pass, CA; 10-year NdPr price floor of $110/kg; 100% magnet off-take for 10 years.
- **Department of War** (formerly DoD) now the lead agency for CRM strategy.

### European Union
- **CRMA** implementation continues — benchmarks for domestic extraction, processing, recycling.
- **Industrial Accelerator Act** (March 4, 2026): "Buy European" / "trusted origin" requirements under discussion.
- **EU-US CMA signed April 24, 2026**: EU-origin extracted/processed minerals count toward IRA EV tax credits. Action Plan for Supply Chain Resilience includes reference price mechanisms, border-adjusted price floors.
- **Key gap**: No ring-fenced CRM budget in current MFF; calls for future CRM Centre with dedicated budget.

### Price Floor Discussions
- Ongoing EU-US talks about minimum price mechanisms/price bands to counter Chinese predatory pricing.
- VP JD Vance proposed "Critical Minerals Preferential Trade Area" with enforceable price floors.

## 5. Technology Watch

### Battery Recycling
- **Rice University** (May 2026): Aqueous amino chloride solutions extract Li, Co, Ni, Mn in minutes at room temperature — ~65% extraction in 1 minute. Eliminates acids and high temperatures.
- **Fraunhofer IFAM** (Germany): Screen-printed electrode system selectively extracts Li, Co, Ni, REEs from battery recycling water — no chemicals, 30-40% more efficient.

### Bio-Engineered Extraction
- **UC Berkeley** (May 2026): Genetically engineered virus that acts as a "smart sponge" selectively grabbing REEs from acid mine drainage. Two proteins: lanthanum-binding peptide + elastin switch (releases REEs when warmed). Tested on real AMD.
- **Chinese Academy of Sciences** (April 2026): 1.4nm artificial ion channels inspired by biological calcium channels — selectively extracts uranium, copper, gold from seawater. 22-day continuous test on natural seawater.

### Mine Waste Recovery
- **Altilium/ReOre Technologies** (Feb 2026): EcoMineral™ process using biodegradable lixiviants to recover copper from tailings — up to 70% direct leaching at near-neutral pH. Targeting Medet tailings (110Mt) in Eastern Europe.
- **Utah-Japan Zero-Waste Partnership**: Electric resistance furnace (ERF) technology to recover tungsten from legacy tailings — runs on renewable electricity, no acid consumption.
- **Italian researchers** (EGU 2026): Magnetic separation recovery of REE, Y, Sc, Li, Ti from granite waste offcuts.

### Dominant Trend
Shift toward **low-temperature, chemical-free or green-chemistry, energy-efficient processes** for secondary recovery from battery waste, mine tailings, and seawater — reducing reliance on primary mining.

### Geopolitical Flashpoints
1. **November 10, 2026** — China REE control suspension expiry (most important)
2. **DRC political stability** — elections and contract audits ongoing
3. **Indonesia nickel policy** — potential for export restrictions on processed nickel
4. **Russia sanctions evolution** — potential easing could unlock REE supply to Europe

### Thematic Observation
The critical minerals landscape is **fully bipolar**: the US-led FORGE/CMA coalition vs. China's supply chain dominance. 2026 is the year of **policy catch-up** — Western governments are deploying massive financial tools ($10B+ Vault, $7.5B Russia, $40B+ US project support) but the 15+ year lead times for new mines and processing capacity mean the supply-demand tension will persist into the 2030s.
