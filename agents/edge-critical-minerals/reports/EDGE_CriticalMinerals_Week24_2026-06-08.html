6. Policy, Regulation and Strategic Context

European Union — RESourceEU Action Plan Implementation Accelerates

RESourceEU Action Plan (Adopted December 2025): The European Commission's flagship plan builds on the Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA) to mobilise up to €3 billion over 12 months for CRM projects. Key instruments include InvestEU (up to €2B), Innovation Fund 2026 (€700M+), Horizon Europe (€593M for circular economy and process innovation), and the EIC Accelerator (€100M for CRM). The plan targets reducing strategic dependencies by up to 50% by 2029. A European Critical Raw Materials Centre is being established in early 2026 to provide market intelligence, coordinate joint purchasing, and manage strategic stockpiling.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: EU Monitoring — RESourceEU ↗

Second Strategic Projects Round — 160+ Applications Received: The European Commission received over 160 applications for the second round of CRMA Strategic Projects, with the cut-off on 15 January 2026. Of these, 95 are from within the EU and 66 from outside. Applications include 75 battery value chain projects and 21 focused on rare earth elements for permanent magnets. The first round selected 60 projects (47 EU + 13 non-EU). Strategic status unlocks accelerated permitting (27 months for extraction, 15 months for processing), access to RESourceEU financing, and offtake certainty. Results of the second round selection are pending.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: 2EU Brussels — Second Strategic Projects Round ↗

EU Stockpiling Initiative — Pilot Scheme Operational Early 2026: Tungsten, rare earths, and gallium have been shortlisted for the EU's first coordinated strategic stockpile. The stockpiling pilot scheme became operational in early 2026 under the European Critical Raw Materials Centre. Ten EU countries are involved, led by Italy, France, and Germany. Talks are underway with major ports including Rotterdam for storage facilities. Magnesium, germanium, and graphite are under consideration for future phases. Member States can count stockpiling contributions toward their 1.5% GDP NATO defence spending commitment.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union / NATO
  • URL: Mining Digital — EU Stockpile ↗

Export Restrictions on Permanent Magnet Scrap and Li-ion Battery Waste (2026): Export restrictions on permanent magnet scrap and aluminium scrap are being implemented in Q2 2026. Lithium-ion battery waste and black mass exports to non-OECD countries will be banned from September 2026. Copper scrap is under assessment for future inclusion. These restrictions aim to retain processing value in Europe and reduce dependency on Chinese recycling infrastructure. EU recyclers could cover up to 20% of permanent magnet demand with adequate feedstock retained in Europe.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Mining Magazine — EU Export Restrictions ↗

Scandinavia — European Rare Earth Hub Emerges

Norway Fen Complex — Government Takes Over Planning (April 2026): Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre announced the Norwegian state will assume responsibility for zoning and permitting of the Fen rare earth deposit in Telemark. The deposit holds 15.9 million tonnes of rare earth oxides (81% increase from 2024 estimates), making it Europe's largest known rare earth deposit. Approximately 19% is neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) critical for permanent magnets. First production targeted for late 2031. A Letter of Intent with Copenhagen Atomics will supply thorium for next-generation molten salt nuclear reactors. Challenges include environmental opposition (78 red-listed species at the processing site) and the need for an operating permit.

  • Jurisdiction: Norway
  • URL: Energy News — Fen Deposit ↗

Norra Kärr Mining Lease — Awaiting Swedish Government Decision: The Swedish Mining Inspectorate has recommended approval of the mining lease for Leading Edge Materials' Norra Kärr heavy rare earth project and submitted it to the Swedish Government for a final decision. Every government agency involved in the permitting process has recommended approval. The deposit is one of Europe's richest HREE sources (dysprosium, terbium) with an initial 26-year mine life. An MoU with Ascension Earth Resources will test innovative proprietary processing technology on eudialyte mineral samples. The company raised CAD $788K through director option exercises in April 2026.

  • Jurisdiction: Sweden
  • URL: Globe and Mail — Norra Kärr ↗

UK–US Critical Minerals MoU (February 2026): The UK and US signed a bilateral Memorandum of Understanding on critical minerals in Washington DC. The agreement covers coordinated investment in mining, separation, and processing projects; joint project selection; streamlined permitting; and cooperation on pricing, recycling, geological mapping, and national security reviews of asset sales. A Mining, Minerals and Metals Investment Ministerial is to convene within 180 days. The MoU complements existing UK bilateral agreements with Australia and Canada.

  • Jurisdiction: United Kingdom / United States

UK Critical Minerals Strategy Targets: Backed by up to £50M in new funding, the strategy targets no more than 60% of any single critical mineral from one country by 2035, at least 10% of annual demand from domestic production, and 20% from recycling. The CLIMATES programme (£15M) supports rare earth supply chains with 16 innovation projects already funded. The UK critical minerals sector contributes £1.79B to the economy and supports over 50,000 jobs with more than 50 active projects.

  • Jurisdiction: United Kingdom
  • URL: UK Government — UK-US Partnership ↗

Geopolitical Context — China's Export Controls Shape Global Markets

China's Expanding Export Controls (2025–2026): China has imposed export licensing on 12 rare earth elements in two tranches (April and October 2025), with extraterritorial reach modelled on the US Foreign Direct Product Rule — items containing as little as 0.1% Chinese-origin content require Chinese export licenses. The October 2025 controls were suspended for one year as part of a Trump-Xi trade truce, with the suspension expiring November 2026. In January 2026, China imposed dual-use export bans targeting Japan, effectively halting heavy rare earth shipments. Yttrium prices have risen approximately 15-fold since controls took effect. Heavy rare earth exports are running at 41–49% of pre-restriction levels. China accounts for ~60% of global mined rare earth supply and ~90% of processing capacity.

  • Jurisdiction: Global / China
  • URL: S&P Global — Rare Earth Bottlenecks ↗

Western Responses to Chinese Supply Dominance: The US Pentagon took a $400M equity stake in MP Materials with price floors and 10-year purchase commitments. "Project Vault" creates a strategic minerals reserve. The G7 is discussing price floors for rare earths to make non-Chinese production economically viable. The EU's RESourceEU plan, stockpiling initiative, and 47+ designated strategic projects represent a coordinated Western response. The European Court of Auditors (February 2026) found that EU diversification efforts have produced "no tangible results" so far, with domestic processing capacity declining due partly to high energy costs. Recycling rates for many critical minerals remain between 1% and 5%.

  • Jurisdiction: Global
  • URL: Gibson Dunn — Resource Nationalism ↗
