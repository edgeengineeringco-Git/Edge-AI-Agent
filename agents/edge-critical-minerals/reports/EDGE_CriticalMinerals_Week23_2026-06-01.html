6. Policy, Regulation and Strategic Context

EU — Critical Raw Materials Act Implementation Accelerates

47 Strategic Projects Designated Under CRMA (May 2026): The European Commission has formally designated 47 strategic projects across 13 EU member states, covering the full CRM value chain from mining through processing to recycling. Northern Europe dominates mining and early processing, while Southern Europe hosts battery-related assets. An additional 13 strategic projects were approved outside the EU, including rare earth projects in Brazil and graphite in Greenland. Combined investment across the 60 strategic projects is approximately €5.5 billion. Strategic status unlocks accelerated permitting, access to the €3B RESourceEU funding, and coordinated support from the European Critical Raw Materials Centre.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Mining South East Europe — 47 Strategic Projects ↗

EU Stockpiling Initiative — Tungsten, Rare Earths, Gallium Shortlisted (May 2026): The EU has shortlisted tungsten, rare earths, and gallium for its first joint strategic stockpile. Talks are underway with major ports including Rotterdam for storage facilities. Ten EU countries are involved, led by Italy, France, and Germany. This is the first coordinated EU-level stockpiling effort for critical minerals, modelled on the US National Defense Stockpile. Magnesium, germanium, and graphite are under consideration for future phases.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: EuroMining News — EU Stockpile Planning ↗

Export Restrictions on Permanent Magnet Scrap and Li-ion Battery Waste (2026): Export restrictions on permanent magnet scrap and aluminium scrap are being implemented in Q2 2026. Lithium-ion battery waste and black mass exports to non-OECD countries will be banned from September 2026. Copper scrap may be added. These restrictions aim to retain processing value in Europe and reduce dependency on Chinese recycling infrastructure.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Mining Journal — €3B EU Spend ↗

UK — Critical Minerals Strategy Advances

UK–US Critical Minerals MoU (February 2026): The UK and US signed a bilateral Memorandum of Understanding on critical minerals in Washington DC. The agreement covers joint investment mobilisation, priority project identification, and streamlined permitting. A Mining, Minerals and Metals Investment Ministerial is to convene within 180 days. The MoU complements existing UK bilateral agreements with Australia and Canada, creating a network of supply chain partnerships.

  • Jurisdiction: United Kingdom / United States
  • URL: UK Government — UK-US Critical Minerals MoU ↗

UK Strategic Targets (2035): The UK Critical Minerals Strategy targets no more than 60% of any single critical mineral from one country, at least 10% of annual demand from domestic production, at least 50,000 tonnes of lithium produced domestically by 2035, and 20% of annual demand from recycling. The strategy is backed by up to £50M in new funding. Over 50 critical mineral projects are now based in the UK, contributing £1.79 billion to the economy and supporting over 50,000 jobs.

  • Jurisdiction: United Kingdom
  • URL: Mining Technology — UK-US Agreement ↗

Nordic — European Rare Earth Hub Emerges

Fen Complex Resource Upgraded to 15.9Mt REO (March 2026): Rare Earths Norway's Fen Complex in Telemark has been reassessed at 15.9 million tonnes of rare earth oxides — approximately 80% larger than the 2024 estimate of 8.8 Mt and one of the largest rare earth deposits globally. The resource contains high-value neodymium and praseodymium for permanent magnets. Production is not expected before the mid-2030s due to permitting and environmental requirements. A proposed "underground invisible mine" design aims to minimise surface impact but increases capital costs.

  • Jurisdiction: Norway
  • URL: Caliber — Fen Complex Resource Upgrade ↗

Norra Kärr Mining Lease Recommended (March 2026): The Swedish Mining Inspectorate has recommended approval of Leading Edge Materials' Norra Kärr heavy rare earth project, submitting it to the Swedish Government for a final decision. The deposit is one of Europe's richest HREE sources (dysprosium, terbium) with an initial 26-year mine life and annual production potential of ~248 tonnes dysprosium oxide and ~36 tonnes terbium oxide. All government agencies involved have recommended approval.

  • Jurisdiction: Sweden
  • URL: MarketScreener — Norra Kärr Lease Recommendation ↗

Geopolitical Context

China controls approximately 70% of global rare earth production and 90% of processing, creating acute supply chain vulnerability for the EU and UK. The EU's designation of 47 strategic projects, joint stockpiling initiative, and export restrictions on scrap materials all reflect a coordinated strategy to reduce this dependency. Restrictions on Chinese entities participating in EU-funded CRM research and innovation are being implemented. The European Critical Raw Materials Centre (operational early 2026) will coordinate joint purchasing, stockpiling, and market intelligence. These dynamics create sustained policy tailwinds for EU-based CRM exploration technology providers like EDGE.

  • Jurisdiction: Global
  • URL: Chambers — RESourceEU Analysis ↗
