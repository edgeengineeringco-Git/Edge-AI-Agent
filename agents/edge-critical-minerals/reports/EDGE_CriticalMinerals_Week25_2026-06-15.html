6. Policy, Regulation and Strategic Context

European Union — CRMA Implementation Accelerates

EU Council Position on CRMA Amendments (March 2026): The EU Council adopted its negotiating position on 4 March 2026, proposing centralised Commission oversight of large companies' CRM supply chains. Key provisions include risk-mitigation measures for single-supplier dependence, Digital Product Passports for permanent magnets, and recycled content alignment with eco-design rules. Negotiations with the European Parliament are pending, with adoption of amendments expected by mid-2027.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: IEU Monitoring — EU Council Position ↗

RESourceEU Action Plan — First Deliverables Underway: The European Critical Raw Materials Centre is being established in 2026, modelled on Japan's JOGMEC, to steer investment, coordinate joint purchasing, and manage strategic stockpiles. The Raw Materials Mechanism matchmaking tool launched November 2025, with the first matchmaking round held in March 2026. Export restrictions on permanent magnet scrap and aluminium scrap are being implemented in Q2 2026. Lithium-ion battery waste exports to non-OECD countries will be banned from September 2026.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: Chambers and Partners — RESourceEU Analysis ↗

Second CRMA Strategic Projects Round — Results Pending: Over 160 applications were received for the second round (cut-off 15 January 2026), including 95 from within the EU and 66 from outside. Of these, 75 projects support the battery value chain and 21 focus on rare earth elements for permanent magnets. The European Commission is still assessing applications and consulting with Member States. Results are expected in mid-2026. Strategic status unlocks accelerated permitting (27 months extraction, 15 months processing), RESourceEU financing access, and offtake certainty.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
  • URL: 2EU Brussels — Second Strategic Projects Round ↗

CRM Board Strategic Stockpiling — First Reporting Deadline 24 May 2026: Member States have submitted their first reports on strategic stocks of critical and strategic raw materials. The European Commission is developing a draft benchmark for safe stockpiling levels. A pilot stockpiling project was launched by several Member States and the Commission in early 2026, with rare earths, tungsten, and gallium shortlisted. EU countries can count stockpiling contributions toward NATO's 1.5% GDP defence spending commitment.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union / NATO
  • URL: Gleiss Lutz — RESourceEU Investor Impact ↗

Scandinavia — European Rare Earth Hub Emerges

Norway Fen Deposit — Government Takes Over Permitting: Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre announced the Norwegian state will assume responsibility for zoning and permitting of the Fen rare earth deposit in Telemark. The deposit holds 15.9 million tonnes of rare earth oxides, making it Europe's largest known REE deposit (~19% NdPr). First production targeted for late 2031. A Letter of Intent with Copenhagen Atomics will supply thorium for next-generation molten salt nuclear reactors. Environmental opposition remains a key challenge.

  • Jurisdiction: Norway
  • URL: Energy News — Fen Deposit ↗

Norra Kärr Mining Lease — Swedish Government Decision Pending: The Swedish Mining Inspectorate has recommended approval of the mining lease for Leading Edge Materials' Norra Kärr HREE project, with every government agency in the permitting process recommending approval. The deposit is one of Europe's richest HREE sources (dysprosium, terbium) with an initial 26-year mine life. An MoU with Ascension Earth Resources will test proprietary processing technology on eudialyte mineral samples.

  • Jurisdiction: Sweden
  • URL: Globe and Mail — Norra Kärr ↗

Geopolitical Context — China's Rare Earth Dominance Intensifies

Yttrium Prices Surge ~140–150x Since April 2025 Controls: China's export licensing on heavy rare earths (April 2025) has caused an extraordinary price surge. Yttrium metal prices rose approximately 69–150 times in ex-China markets. Exports of yttrium, dysprosium, and terbium are running at just 42%, 41%, and 49% respectively of pre-restriction volumes. The October 2025 extraterritorial controls were suspended for one year (expires November 2026), but the April 2025 controls remain fully in force. Some US aerospace companies have temporarily paused production due to yttrium shortages.

  • Jurisdiction: Global / China
  • URL: Yahoo Finance — China's Rare Earth Grip Holds ↗

China's New Mineral Resources Law Regulations (June 2026): China enacted new Mineral Resources Law regulations introducing national security reviews for foreign mining investments and fast-tracking strategic mineral reserve buildout. This signals even tighter full-chain oversight extending beyond rare earths to other critical minerals. The US Pentagon has taken a $400M equity stake in MP Materials, and $1.6B has been committed to USA Rare Earth for a Texas deposit and processing facility. The US NDAA will prohibit federal purchase of Chinese-processed rare earth materials from 2027 onward.

  • Jurisdiction: Global
  • URL: Canadian Mining Journal — China Mining Controls ↗

Implementation Challenges Persist in EU CRM Strategy: A joint policy brief from 11 Horizon Europe projects highlights that Europe's main risk is not resource scarcity but delayed implementation. Key bottlenecks include financing gaps for first-of-a-kind plants, fragmented regulatory practices, permitting complexity, and social acceptance challenges. The European Court of Auditors (February 2026) found that EU diversification efforts have produced "no tangible results" so far, with domestic processing capacity declining.

  • Jurisdiction: European Union
