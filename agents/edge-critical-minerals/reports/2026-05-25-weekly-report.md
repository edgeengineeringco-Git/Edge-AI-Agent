# EDGE Critical Minerals Intel — Weekly Brief
**Date:** 2026-05-25
**Report Period:** May 18–24, 2026

## Executive Summary

- **Lithium surges 7.3% in May** as supply constraints from Zimbabwe quotas and China's Jiangxi mine shutdowns collide with surging ESS demand (~31% of total Li offtake). Morgan Stanley forecasts ¥250k/ton peak in Q4 2026; UBS sees ¥298k/ton.
- **Cobalt prices spike ~160%** since DRC export quotas began, with electrolytic cobalt at ~$77,000/ton. Indonesia's ~30% nickel mining quota cut and Eramet's Weda Bay suspension add further battery metals supply pressure.
- **Copper faces a "triple demand threat"** from AI data centres (~475kt demand in 2026), EV scaling, and grid modernisation. Consensus deficit of 150–330kt, with spot TC/RC fees turning negative (minus $70/ton) — smelters paying mines for ore access.
- **US launches "Project Vault"** — a $120B strategic critical minerals stockpile — while pressing allies to adopt minimum pricing and tariff walls against Chinese-sourced minerals. US deployed ~€40B in CRM support since 2021, roughly 8x the EU.
- **Resource nationalism reaches new intensity**: DRC (cobalt quotas), Indonesia (nickel mining cuts + pricing reforms), Zimbabwe (lithium export quotas), and China (expanded rare earth export restrictions) are simultaneously tightening supply.

## 1. Market Movements

### Lithium
- **Spot battery-grade Li₂CO₃**: ~$22,970–23,000/ton (¥200k–230k/ton), +7.3% in May, +104% YTD.
- **Supply tightness**: Zimbabwe's concentrate export quotas have removed 20–30kt from the market. China's Jianxiawo (Yichun) lepidolite mine restart pushed to Q4 2026; 7 more Jiangxi mines at risk. Greenbushes (Australia) cut annual guidance.
- **Demand**: China May battery output +10.5% MoM (+66% YoY); LFP +16% MoM (+72% YoY). ESS now accounts for ~31% of lithium demand, up from 23%.
- **Outlook divergence**: UBS (bullish, ¥298k target) vs. Goldman Sachs (H1 2026 peak, decline to $10–16k by 2028). Morgan Stanley sees Q4 spike to ¥250k but long-term rational price at ~¥100k.

### Rare Earths
- **China expanded restricted export list** to 12 medium/heavy REEs (holmium, erbium, thulium, europium, ytterbium added) with extraterritorial reach — foreign producers using Chinese-sourced materials or equipment must obtain Chinese export licenses.
- **No liquid price benchmark** for most REEs, but NdPr prices remain elevated on US defense stockpiling and magnet supply chain premiums (4–6x for ex-China processed material).
- **Separation remains the true bottleneck**: China controls ~90% of global separation capacity. Barriers to non-China capacity: high capital requirements, multi-year qualification, environmental scrutiny.

### Cobalt & Nickel
- **Cobalt**: Electrolytic cobalt at ~$77,000/ton (+8% MTD). DRC's 96,600-ton annual export quota represents a ~55% cut from 2024 actual exports. Cobalt hydroxide prices have doubled. Supply deficit of 6,000–25,000 tons projected for 2026.
- **Nickel**: LME nickel ~$18,400/ton (+1% MTD). Indonesia cut mining quotas ~30% (379M → 260–270M tons). Eramet's Weda Bay mine ceased production mid-May after RKAB quota slashed 71%. China's sulfur export ban (May 1) threatens HPAL processing costs (+$1,000–3,000/ton).
- **Battery chemistry shift**: LFP now 81.3% of China's power battery installations, reducing cobalt intensity. High-nickel chemistries remain essential for long-range EVs.

### Copper
- **Price**: ~$13,000/ton (~$5.90/lb), near record highs. Consensus full-year deficit of 150–330kt.
- **Supply crisis deepening**: Spot TC/RC fees negative (minus $70/ton). Grasberg (force majeure), Kamoa-Kakula (earthquake halved Q1 output), El Teniente (tunnel collapse). Sulfuric acid crisis — Strait of Hormuz tensions disrupted ~50% of seaborne sulfur flows; China halted sulfuric acid exports.
- **Demand**: AI data centres (~475kt in 2026, +110kt YoY), EVs (3–4x copper/vehicle vs. ICE), grid modernisation (~60% of demand growth). S&P Global projects 50% demand increase by 2040.

### Graphite & Battery Minerals
- **Market oversupply persists** despite strong demand growth. Natural flake graphite prices remain depressed; synthetic graphite prices rising on tight supply and rising coke costs.
- **China controls ~78% of natural graphite output, ~90% of anode production, ~98% of graphitization capacity.** US anti-dumping duty of 93.5% on Chinese anode-grade graphite (since July 2025) has not yet shifted procurement patterns meaningfully.
- **Developments**: NextSource Materials signed supply deal with Syrah for Abu Dhabi BAF (34–68kt fines over 7 years). Northern Graphite (Quebec) moved spot sales to Metalshub. Building anode plants outside China costs 3–10x more.
- **Sodium-ion batteries** (hard-carbon anodes, no graphite) are scaling — CATL targeting 40 GWh by year-end 2026 — which could reduce graphite demand intensity at the margin.

## 2. Geopolitical Developments

### US-China Rivalry
- **"Project Vault"** — US launched a $120B strategic critical minerals reserve. USTR Jamieson Greer proposed minimum pricing mechanisms and tariff walls against Chinese-sourced minerals.
- **US pressuring allies** to pay a "national security premium" for non-Chinese supply chains.
- **China's 15th Five-Year Plan** expected to prioritise strategic materials self-sufficiency, potentially reducing technology transfer overseas.
- **Bifurcated markets emerging**: Western-aligned supply tracks (Australia, Canada, US, Brazil) vs. China-aligned tracks (Indonesia, DRC, Myanmar), with pricing reflecting jurisdictional risk rather than pure production cost.

### Russia-Ukraine
- FY 2026 NDAA included new sanctions authorities targeting critical mineral supply chains linked to Russia.
- US pursuing access to Ukraine's critical mineral reserves (uranium, REEs, lithium, titanium in Donbas) as part of broader resource strategy.
- Strait of Hormuz blockade (Iran conflict, early 2026) demonstrated energy chokepoint vulnerability — oil above $100/bbl, European gas prices doubled.

### Resource Nationalism
- **Synchronised export restrictions**: DRC (cobalt quotas), Indonesia (nickel mining cuts + pricing reform), Zimbabwe (lithium quotas), and China (REE export controls) represent a structural shift toward resource nationalism.
- **OECD reports export restrictions on critical minerals increased ~5x between 2009–2024.**
- Producer countries (Zambia, South Africa, Brazil) leveraging competition between China and the West for better terms — technology transfer, infrastructure, local processing.

## 3. Policy & Regulation

### United States
- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025)** established $5B Industrial Base Fund, $2B National Defense Stockpile Fund, and up to $500M/project Critical Minerals Loan Program.
- **Defense Production Act**: $550.4M in FY2025 aid (up from $209.2M in FY2024). 10 companies supported since January 2025.
- **MP Materials landmark deal**: DoD $400M investment + $150M loan + 10-year $110/kg NdPr price floor + 100% magnet purchase commitment for 10 years.
- **January 2026 proclamation**: 180-day deadline (to July 13, 2026) for processed critical mineral import trade deals or face tariffs/quota.
- **Critical minerals list expanded to 60**, adding boron, copper, lead, metallurgical coal, phosphates, potash, rhenium, silicon, silver, uranium.

### European Union
- **CRMA Strategic Projects**: 47 within EU (€22.5B capital required), 13 in non-EU countries (€5.5B). Applications for second call closed January 15, 2026.
- **RESourceEU Action Plan**: €3B investment drive — €2B EIB, €300M Battery Booster, €600M Horizon Europe.
- **Industrial Accelerator Act (March 2026)**: Opens door to "Buy European" procurement requirements.
- **FDI Screening**: Revision of EU FDI Regulation under consideration — mandatory screening for critical minerals.
- **Key gap**: EU deployed ~€5B vs. US ~€40B in CRM support (IFRI, March 2026). EU lacks equivalents to US DFC and EXIM for equity stakes.

### Asia-Pacific
- **Australia**: FIRB increasing scrutiny of Chinese-linked investments in critical minerals. US-Australia framework: $1B committed to REE projects.
- **Japan**: Model of joint purchasing and strategic stockpiling being adopted by EU.
- **Saudi Arabia & UAE**: Increasing investment in global critical mineral assets, adding competitive pressure.

## 4. Supply Chain Intelligence

### Production Disruptions
- **Eramet Weda Bay (Indonesia)**: Production ceased mid-May — RKAB quota cut 71% (32M → 12M wmt).
- **Grasberg (Indonesia)**: Force majeure since September 2025; 2026 production expected 1/3 lower.
- **Kamoa-Kakula (DRC)**: Earthquake halved Q1 2026 copper output.
- **El Teniente (Chile)**: Tunnel collapse forced suspension.
- **Jianxiawo mine (Jiangxi, China)**: Restart delayed to Q4 2026; 7 more lepidolite mines at risk.

### Processing Bottlenecks
- **Sulfuric acid crisis**: Strait of Hormuz disruptions + China export ban threatens SX-EW copper output (~17% of global supply) and HPAL nickel processing.
- **Smelter margin crisis**: Copper TC/RC fees at historic lows (negative spot). Record low 2026 annual contracts (Antofagasta–Jiangxi Copper at zero).
- **Graphite processing**: Building anode capacity outside China costs 3–10x more. Qualification timelines span years.
- **REE separation**: Non-China capacity faces 5–10 year build timelines. US DoD invested $439M+ since 2020.

### Company News
- **CATL**: Scaling battery recycling (99.6% Ni/Co/Mn recovery), established $42B mining resources group, targeting 40 GWh sodium-ion by end-2026.
- **SQM**: Q1 earnings due May 26. Deutsche Bank upgraded to Buy (PT $88). Stock +18% YTD.
- **MP Materials**: DoD-backed $400M investment for domestic magnet production ("10X Facility").
- **Boliden**: SEK 4.8B new tankhouse at Rönnskär smelter (230kt copper capacity) — full operations Q4 2026.
- **Altilium**: Filed patent for EcoMineral™ green hydrometallurgical process (copper from tailings). Established ReOre Technologies.
- **Electros Inc. (OTC)**: Emerging Africa-focused lithium miner; bullish on elevated energy prices.

## 5. Technology Watch

### Recycling Innovations
- **Nanomox OIS® (UK)**: Pilot-scale REE oxide recovery from waste magnets. Raised £2.4M for commercial plant.
- **Supra Elemental Recovery (UT Austin)**: 3D-printed reusable cartridge filters critical minerals at molecular level. $250K seed funding.
- **Altilium EcoMineral™**: Green hydrometallurgical process achieves up to 70% copper leaching efficiency from 110M-tonne tailings site.
- **REEVIVE (Finland, 2026–2028)**: Inventory and recovery of REEs from secondary sources targeting EU 25% recycling mandate.
- **NETL (US DOE)**: Step-extraction processes for REE/Co/Ni/Sc from coal ash and acid mine drainage. Barrel-scale pilot complete.

### Substitution & Alternative Processing
- **DARPA SMASH Program**: 48-month R&D to recover all ~80 stable elements from ore with near-zero waste using microbial fluids, plasma thrusters, and Faraday cups.
- **PNNL**: Nickel extraction from olivine using waste acid at room temperature — +$24/ton net gain vs. conventional.
- **Furnace Japan ERF (Utah-Japan partnership)**: Electric resistance furnace recovers tungsten from tailings using electricity instead of acid. Zero-waste slag. Pilot plant planned in Milford, Utah.
- **Sodium-ion batteries**: CATL scaling to 40 GWh by end-2026. Hard-carbon anodes eliminate graphite and reduce lithium intensity.

### Exploration Tech
- **Magnetic separation from granite waste (Italy)**: Recovery of REEs, Y, Sc, Li from extractive waste — turning by-product into CRM source.
- **Supercritical fluid extraction (WashU)**: scCO₂/scN₂/scAir processes achieve nearly 100% purity REE recovery from coal fly ash.

## 6. Outlook & Risk Assessment

### Key Risks to Monitor Next Week
1. **SQM Q1 earnings (May 26)** — Will signal lithium demand trajectory and South American supply outlook.
2. **US critical mineral import tariff deadline (July 13)** — 180-day review period approaching; potential for new tariffs on processed Chinese minerals.
3. **Indonesia supplemental nickel quotas** — Potential Q3 2026 issuance could ease supply, capping nickel price upside.
4. **China 15th Five-Year Plan details** — Anticipated strategic materials priorities may further tighten export controls.
5. **Strait of Hormuz / sulfuric acid** — If disruptions persist, copper SX-EW output (~17% of global) faces significant curtailment.

### Price Forecast Signals
- **Lithium**: Bullish near-term (¥200–250k); medium-term divergence between structural deficit view (UBS/MS) and peak-cycle view (Goldman).
- **Cobalt**: Strongly bullish — supply deficit, limited substitution options, DRC quota regime entrenched.
- **Nickel**: Moderately bullish — policy cuts offset by high LME inventories. Watch for supplemental Indonesian quotas.
- **Copper**: Structurally bullish — deficit consensus, negative TC/FCs, AI/electrification demand. Goldman outlier at $10,700/t; Citi at $13,000/t as early as Q2.
- **Graphite**: Near-term oversupply masks structural tightness. Critical milestone deadlines in 2026/2027 for ex-China projects.

### Geopolitical Flashpoints
- **US-China**: Project Vault and potential July tariffs escalate mineral trade war. China retains processing stranglehold for 5–10 years minimum.
- **Resource nationalism cluster**: DRC, Indonesia, Zimbabwe — simultaneous restrictions create compounding battery metals supply shock.
- **Middle East**: Strait of Hormuz blockade cascading into sulfuric acid → copper production crisis.
- **EU-US coordination gap**: US deployed 8x EU CRM funding. Unless EU mobilises significant capital, risks falling further behind in supply security.
