# EDGE Critical Minerals Intel — Weekly Brief
**Date:** 2026-05-25
**Report Period:** May 18–24, 2026

## Executive Summary

- **Lithium breaks above ¥200k/ton** (near-3-year high) as supply constraints from Zimbabwe export quotas, Jiangxi mine disruptions, and Greenbushes output cuts collide with surging ESS demand (~31% of total Li offtake, up from 23%). CITIC Securities targets ¥250k/ton in 2–3 months; UBS sees ¥298k/ton.
- **Cobalt prices surge >160%** since DRC export quotas began; electrolytic cobalt at ~$77,000/ton. Indonesia's ~30% nickel mining quota cut and Eramet's Weda Bay shutdown (mid-May) compound battery metals supply shock. China's sulfuric acid export ban (May 1) threatens HPAL processing costs.
- **Copper faces structural deficit** — spot TC/RC fees at negative $45–70/ton (smelters paying mines). Consensus 2026 deficit of 400–490kt. AI data centres (~475kt demand), EV scaling, and grid modernisation driving a "triple demand threat."
- **US launches "Project Vault"** ($120B strategic stockpile) while pressing allies on minimum pricing/tariff walls. US deployed ~€40B in CRM support (8x EU). Trump-Xi summit (May 14–15) delivered tactical pause only — rare earth controls remain.
- **Resource nationalism intensifies on five fronts**: DRC (cobalt quotas), Indonesia (nickel cuts + pricing reform), Zimbabwe (lithium quotas), China (expanded REE controls to 12 elements), and US (Venezuela minerals license excluding China/Russia).

## 1. Market Movements

### Lithium
- **Spot battery-grade Li₂CO₃**: GFEX futures hit ~¥205,260/ton (new YTD high); spot at ¥195,000–195,650/ton (+10.3% YTD). Spodumene (CIF China) at ~$2,985–3,040/ton — highest since late 2023.
- **Supply tightness**: Zimbabwe concentrate export quotas have removed 20–30kt (no ore arriving until late July). China's Jianxiawo (Yichun) restart delayed to Q4 2026; 4 clay mines entering license change (7,500 t/y LCE at risk). Greenbushes (Australia) FY2026 guidance cut by ~20kt LCE. Chilean lithium salt exports fell 5.1% MoM in April.
- **Demand**: China April battery output 183.9 GWh (+55.6% YoY); May schedules +10.5% MoM (LFP +16% MoM, +72% YoY). ESS now ~31% of lithium demand (up from 23%). China EV exports +111.8% YoY in April.
- **Inventories**: Chinese inventories at ~103kt (only 3 weeks demand); ~10kt drawdown expected in May alone. Refinery stocks critically low at ~19kt.
- **Outlook**: CITIC Securities ¥250k/ton in 2–3 months; UBS ¥298k/ton (+50% upside); Morgan Stanley sees Q4 spike to ¥250k. Goldman Sachs diverges — expects H1 2026 peak then decline to $10–16k by 2028.

### Rare Earths
- **China expanded restricted export list to 12 REEs** (May 12, 2026): holmium, erbium, thulium, europium, ytterbium added with extraterritorial reach — foreign entities using Chinese tech/materials need export licenses. "Unreliable entities list" targets US defense firms.
- **Trump-Xi summit (May 14–15)** produced no rare earth concessions — China "will address concerns" statement viewed as tactical, not structural. November 2026: one-year suspension of Oct 2025 controls expires.
- **Separation remains the real bottleneck**: China controls ~90% of global capacity. Non-China barriers: capital intensity (hundreds of millions $), multi-year qualification, environmental scrutiny.
- **Technology**: Silex (UK) launched modular micro-refineries for REE recovery from industrial waste streams (alkali-based + low-energy conversion). ReElement Technologies scaling chromatographic separation (Marion, IN; Q3 2026 target).
- **NdPr prices**: Elevated on US defense stockpiling; ex-China processed material commands 4–6x premium.

### Cobalt & Nickel
- **Cobalt**: Electrolytic cobalt at ~$77,000/ton (+8% MTD, >160% since DRC quotas). DRC's 96,600-ton annual export quota (~55% cut from 2024). Cobalt hydroxide prices have doubled. Sulfuric acid crisis compounds risk: China banned H₂SO₄ exports (May 1), threatening DRC copper-cobalt wet processing. Supply deficit of 6,000–25,000 tons projected for 2026.
- **Nickel**: LME nickel ~$18,500–20,000/ton (up sharply since mid-April). Indonesia mining quotas cut ~30% (379M → 260–270M wmt). **Eramet Weda Bay ceased production mid-May** after RKAB quota slashed 71% (32M → 12M wmt). New pricing formula (Decree No. 144) raises low-grade ore coefficients 17% → 30%, taxing associated Co, Fe, Cr even if unrecovered.
- **Vale Indonesia CEO warning**: negative ESG campaigns could put Indonesian nickel in same position as Congolese cobalt — risking buyer flight.
- **Battery chemistry shift**: LFP now 81.3% of China's power battery installations. High-nickel chemistries remain essential for long-range EVs.

### Copper
- **Price**: ~$13,500–14,500/ton (LME, near record highs). Consensus 2026 deficit of 400–490kt — sharp escalation from ~185kt in 2025.
- **TC/RC crisis**: 2026 annual benchmark settled at **$0/ton** (Antofagasta–Chinese smelters). Spot rates at **negative $45–70/ton** — smelters effectively paying mines for ore. Global idle smelting capacity at 14.3% (up from 8.2%).
- **Supply disruptions**: Grasberg (force majeure, ~1/3 lower 2026 output), Kamoa-Kakula (earthquake halved Q1), El Teniente (tunnel collapse). Sulfuric acid crisis: Strait of Hormuz tensions disrupted ~50% of seaborne sulfur flows + China export ban threatens SX-EW output (~17% of global Cu).
- **Demand**: AI data centres ~475kt in 2026 (+110kt YoY), EVs (3–4x Cu vs. ICE), grid modernisation. S&P Global projects 50% demand increase by 2040 (28M → 42M tonnes).
- **New mine delays**: Marimaca (Chile) construction only Q1 2026, production 2028+. Santa Cruz (Arizona) similarly delayed. Average new mine: 17+ years from discovery to production.

### Graphite & Battery Minerals
- **Market oversupply persists** despite strong demand growth. Natural flake graphite prices remain depressed; synthetic graphite prices rising on tight supply and rising coke costs.
- **China controls ~78% of natural graphite output, ~90% of anode production, ~98% of graphitization capacity.** US anti-dumping duty of 93.5% on Chinese anode-grade graphite (since July 2025) has not yet shifted procurement patterns meaningfully.
- **Developments**: NextSource Materials signed supply deal with Syrah for Abu Dhabi BAF (34–68kt fines over 7 years). Northern Graphite (Quebec) moved spot sales to Metalshub. Building anode plants outside China costs 3–10x more.
- **Sodium-ion batteries** (hard-carbon anodes, no graphite) are scaling — CATL targeting 40 GWh by year-end 2026 — which could reduce graphite demand intensity at the margin.

## 2. Geopolitical Developments

### US-China Rivalry
- **Trump-Xi summit (May 14–15, Beijing)**: Tactical stabilisation, not structural reset. China maintained rare earth export controls; US escalated Project Vault and African REE project financing.
- **"Minerals NATO"** : US leading 28-country coalition building exclusionary strategic resource supply system. Analysts: governance weaknesses in Africa, resource nationalism may limit effectiveness. Could raise REE/Li/Ga/Ge prices 30–50%.
- **"Project Vault"** — US $120B strategic reserve. USTR Greer pushing minimum pricing mechanisms and tariff walls against Chinese-sourced minerals.
- **US pressuring allies** to pay a "national security premium" for non-Chinese supply chains.
- **China's 15th Five-Year Plan** expected to prioritise strategic materials self-sufficiency, potentially reducing technology transfer overseas.

### Russia-Ukraine & Iran
- FY 2026 NDAA: new sanctions authorities targeting critical mineral supply chains linked to Russia.
- US pursuing access to Ukraine's CRM reserves (U, REEs, Li, Ti in Donbas) as part of broader resource strategy.
- **Venezuela**: US Treasury issued "general license" (April 2026) authorising US investment in Venezuelan minerals — **explicitly excluding China, Russia, and 3 other nations**. Venezuela holds 15% of global Li reserves.
- Strait of Hormuz blockade (Iran conflict, early 2026): oil above $100/bbl, European gas prices doubled. Cascading into sulfuric acid crisis affecting base metals processing.

### Resource Nationalism
- **Synchronised export restrictions**: DRC (cobalt quotas), Indonesia (nickel mining cuts + pricing reform), Zimbabwe (lithium quotas), and China (REE export controls) represent a structural shift toward resource nationalism.
- **OECD reports export restrictions on critical minerals increased ~5x between 2009–2024.**
- Producer countries (Zambia, South Africa, Brazil) leveraging competition between China and the West for better terms — technology transfer, infrastructure, local processing.

- **Critical minerals list expanded to 60**: boron, copper, lead, metallurgical coal, phosphates, potash, rhenium, silicon, silver, uranium added.
- **21 bilateral CRM frameworks/MoUs** signed since Trump's return; 17 more under negotiation.

### Asia-Pacific

- **Saudi Arabia & UAE**: Increasing investment in global critical mineral assets, adding competitive pressure.

## 5. Technology Watch

### Recycling Innovations
- **INL EC-Leach (March 2026)**: Recovers >95% Li, Co, Ni, Mn from batteries using electricity instead of harsh chemicals — 75% less energy, 88–90% less chemicals. E-RECOV for e-scrap at room temperature.
- **Valor (ex-Glencore, UIUC)**: Electrochemical liquid-liquid extraction using reusable molecular "ligands" activated/deactivated by electricity. Covers Au, Cu, Pt, Ni, REEs (including Dy, Ce, Y) — 43 more in development. First facility in Houston; "no smelter needed."
- **Supra Elemental Recovery (UT Austin)**: 3D-printed reusable molecular-sponge cartridges for Co, Li, Ga, Sc from tailings and e-waste. $250K seed funding.
- **Altilium EcoMineral™**: Green hydrometallurgy — up to 70% Cu leaching efficiency from 110M-tonne tailings. Patent filed Feb 2026; ReOre Technologies established.
- **U.S. Critical Materials & Columbia University (April 2026)**: "Mud to Metal" — sponsored research recovering Ga, Sc, Ti, REEs from red mud (aluminum refining waste).
- **Nanomox OIS® (UK)**: Pilot-scale REE oxide recovery from waste magnets. £2.4M raised for commercial plant.

### Substitution & Alternative Processing
- **DARPA SMASH Program**: 48-month R&D to recover all ~80 stable elements from ore with near-zero waste using microbial fluids, plasma thrusters, and Faraday cups.
- **China bio-inspired membrane separation (CAS, April 2026)**: Green extraction using microscopic channels inspired by biological ion transport — demonstrated selective recovery from complex environments including seawater.
- **PNNL**: Nickel extraction from olivine using waste acid at room temperature — +$24/ton net gain vs. conventional.
- **Al-for-Cu substitution debate (Critical Minerals '26 conference)**: ~70% of 2023 copper consumption replaceable by aluminium without major performance penalties. Barrier: entrenched reputation, not technical feasibility.
- **Sodium-ion batteries**: CATL scaling to 40 GWh by end-2026. Hard-carbon anodes eliminate graphite, reduce lithium intensity.

### Exploration Tech
- **Silex modular micro-refineries (UK)**: REE recovery from industrial waste streams at source — alkali-based + low-energy conversion. Spin-out from University of Leeds.
- **Magnetic separation from granite waste (Italy, EGU 2026)**: Recovery of REEs, Y, Sc, Li from extractive waste.
- **Supercritical fluid extraction (WashU)**: scCO₂/scN₂/scAir processes achieve nearly 100% purity REE recovery from coal fly ash.
- **Idaho Strategic Resources (May 2026)**: $1M DOE award for REE (Nd, Pr, Dy) exploration and extraction from Idaho-sourced minerals.

## 6. Outlook & Risk Assessment

### Key Risks to Monitor Next Week
1. **SQM Q1 earnings (May 26)** — Will signal lithium demand trajectory and South American supply outlook.
2. **US critical mineral import tariff deadline (July 13)** — 180-day review period approaching; potential for new tariffs on processed Chinese minerals.
3. **Indonesia supplemental nickel quotas** — Potential Q3 2026 issuance could ease supply, capping nickel price upside.
4. **China 15th Five-Year Plan details** — Anticipated strategic materials priorities may further tighten export controls.
5. **Strait of Hormuz / sulfuric acid** — If disruptions persist, copper SX-EW output (~17% of global) faces significant curtailment.

### Price Forecast Signals
- **Lithium**: Bullish near-term (¥200–250k); medium-term divergence between structural deficit view (UBS/MS) and peak-cycle view (Goldman).
- **Cobalt**: Strongly bullish — supply deficit, limited substitution options, DRC quota regime entrenched.
- **Nickel**: Moderately bullish — policy cuts offset by high LME inventories. Watch for supplemental Indonesian quotas.
- **Copper**: Structurally bullish — deficit consensus, negative TC/FCs, AI/electrification demand. Goldman outlier at $10,700/t; Citi at $13,000/t as early as Q2.
- **Graphite**: Near-term oversupply masks structural tightness. Critical milestone deadlines in 2026/2027 for ex-China projects.

### Geopolitical Flashpoints
- **US-China**: Project Vault and potential July tariffs escalate mineral trade war. China retains processing stranglehold for 5–10 years minimum.
- **Resource nationalism cluster**: DRC, Indonesia, Zimbabwe — simultaneous restrictions create compounding battery metals supply shock.
- **Middle East**: Strait of Hormuz blockade cascading into sulfuric acid → copper production crisis.
- **EU-US coordination gap**: US deployed 8x EU CRM funding. Unless EU mobilises significant capital, risks falling further behind in supply security.
