# EDGE Critical Minerals Intel — Weekly Brief
**Date:** 2026-05-25
**Week:** May 19–25, 2026

## Executive Summary

- **Lithium prices hit nearly 3-year highs** above ¥200,000/tonne, driven by supply contraction (Greenbushes cuts, Zimbabwe export ban, Chinese lepidolite risks) and surging energy-storage demand. CITIC and UBS forecast further upside to ¥250,000–300,000/t.
- **Cobalt prices surged 160%+ since DRC export quotas** (~55% cut to 96.6kt/yr); nickel supported by Indonesia's ~30% mining quota reduction but capped by high LME inventories at ~$18,400/t.
- **China expanded rare earth export controls** to 12 elements, extended technology ban extraterritorially (effective Dec 2026), and retains ~90% of global separation capacity — Japan's dependence rebounded to 76%.
- **Copper faces structural deficit** (330–400kt forecast), with AI data centers, defense, and electrification driving "triple demand shock" against depleted ore grades and a decade of underinvestment.
- **Resource nationalism accelerates:** Congo (cobalt), Indonesia (nickel), Zimbabwe (lithium), and China (sulfuric acid export ban) are asserting supply control, while the US and EU escalate counter-strategies through stockpiles, bilateral deals, and the new US-EU Action Plan.

## 1. Market Movements

### Lithium
Spot battery-grade lithium carbonate surged past **¥200,000–205,000/tonne (~$28,000–$29,000)**, the highest since late 2023. The GFEX LC2609 futures contract closed at ¥205,260/t on May 12. Supply-side forces dominate: **Greenbushes** cut FY2026 guidance by ~20kt LCE; **Zimbabwe's** raw lithium export ban (Feb 2026) has yet to yield actual shipments; and **Yichun lepidolite** mines face license renewal risks affecting ~7,500 t LCE/year. On the demand side, May battery production is forecast at a record **249 GWh**, with energy storage now representing 42.3% of cell output (up from 23%). Inventories sit at just 3 weeks' worth (~103kt). CITIC Securities forecasts ¥250,000/t within 2–3 months; UBS sees a further 50% upside to ~$41,875/t.

### Rare Earths
Prices remained elevated amid China's expanded export controls. Beijing added **4 new elements** (holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium) to its restricted list, bringing the total to 12. From December 2026, foreign entities using Chinese rare earth technology or equipment will also require export licenses. Non-China supply remains marginal: **MP Materials** outputs ~40kt total REO (mostly light); **Lynas** achieved commercial heavy rare earth separation in hundreds-of-tonnes scale vs China's hundreds-of-thousands. Japan's import dependence on China has rebounded to **76%**. Heavy rare earths (dysprosium, terbium) remain the most constrained.

### Cobalt & Nickel
Cobalt: Electrolytic cobalt in China reached **¥558,000/tonne (~$77,000/t)** — up over 160% since the DRC's February 2025 export quota of 96.6kt/yr. The DRC cut represents a ~55% reduction vs 2024 export levels. A 2026 supply deficit of 0.6–2.5 Mt is projected. H2 2026 prices may ease to ¥480,000–580,000/t as recycling ramps and LFP chemistry penetration deepens (now 81.3% of China EV installations).

Nickel: LME nickel trades at **~$18,400–18,500/t**, supported by Indonesia cutting mining quotas from 379Mt to 260–270Mt (~30% reduction). Sulfur price spikes have raised HPAL costs by $1,000–3,000/t. However, high LME inventories cap upside. Goldman Sachs raised its 2026 average forecast to $18,500/t. Battery-grade nickel demand is projected to triple by 2030.

### Copper
Copper holds at **~$12,630/tonne ($5.73/lb)** with a consensus 2026 deficit of 330,000–400,000 tonnes. The market faces a "triple demand shock": AI data centers (adding ~110kt demand in 2026), grid electrification (~60% of growth), and defense stockpiling. Supply constraints are severe: ore grades below 0.6%, treatment charges at zero, major disruptions at Grasberg (force majeure), Kamoa-Kakula (earthquake), and El Teniente (tunnel collapse). The Strait of Horumuz-linked sulfuric acid crisis has hit Chilean SX-EW output. Inventories sit below 3 weeks of consumption.

### Graphite & Battery Minerals
Artificial graphite prices rose steadily through May on tight effective capacity and rising coke feedstock costs. Natural graphite anode prices remained range-bound. Supply constraints are emerging as the next battery bottleneck: spherical graphite prices have surged **over 180% since early 2024**. China controls ~90% of global anode production and ~98% of graphitization capacity. US anti-dumping duties (93.5% on Chinese anode graphite) and EU CBAM are reshaping sourcing. Talga's Graphite ONE in Sweden and Graphite One's Ohio plant ($435M, targeting 10kt/yr by end-2027) represent nascent non-China alternatives.

## 2. Geopolitical Developments

### US-China Strategic Competition
The central axis of critical minerals geopolitics continues to intensify. The US launched a **Critical Minerals Preferential Trade Bloc** in February 2026 with 55 countries to coordinate supply chains and reduce Chinese dependence. President Trump announced a $12 billion critical minerals stockpile ("Project Vault"). China counters with expanding export controls on rare earths, gallium, germanium, and — critically — **banned sulfuric acid exports in April 2026**, threatening an estimated 200kt of Chilean copper production. China's 15th Five-Year Plan signals sustained strategic material demand across new energy, aerospace, and 6G.

### Resource Nationalism
A defining trend of 2026: the DRC (cobalt quotas), Indonesia (nickel ore ban, 30% quota cut), Zimbabwe (lithium export ban), and Chile (copper smuggling crackdown) are asserting sovereignty over mineral resources. The OECD reports global critical mineral export restrictions have increased roughly **five-fold** between 2009 and 2024. The African Union's Green Minerals Strategy aims to align mineral development with domestic industrialization.

### Russia-Ukraine & Iran
The Russia-Ukraine conflict is increasingly framed as a resource war — Ukraine's Donbas region holds substantial lithium, rare earths, and titanium. The Iran conflict disrupting sulfur flows through the Strait of Hormuz has created a chemical supply shock affecting copper, nickel, and uranium processing.

## 3. Policy & Regulation

### United States
The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act** (July 2025) modified IRA credits but authorized $5B for an Industrial Base Fund, $2B for the National Defense Stockpile, and $500M for Defense Credit Programs. The Defense Production Act deployed $550.4M in FY2025 for CRM projects. The US has signed **21 bilateral critical mineral frameworks** since January 2025 (Ukraine, DRC, Australia, Japan, Kazakhstan, among others). DOE loans supported MP Materials ($400M DoD + $150M loan), Lithium Americas ($2.3B), and Trilogy Metals (10% equity stake).

### European Union
The **Critical Raw Materials Act** has selected 60 Strategic Projects (47 internal, 13 external) requiring €22.5B and €5.5B respectively. The **ReSourceEU Action Plan** (2026) targets €3B investment through a new European Critical Raw Materials Centre. EIB/EBRD committed €2B for CRM investment in 2025. The **US-EU Action Plan for Critical Minerals Supply Chain Resilience** (April 24, 2026) established a framework for reference pricing, stockpiling cooperation, and investment screening.

### Asia-Pacific
Japan's dependence on Chinese rare earths rebounded to 76%, highlighting the failure of diversification efforts. Australia signed bilateral minerals agreements with the US and is negotiating EU-backed equity stakes. South Korea announced expanded stockpiling of lithium and rare earths.

### Production & Processing Bottlenecks
- **Lithium**: Greenbushes production cut; Zimbabwe zero shipments; Chinese lepidolite at risk; Bald Hill and Finniss restarts possible but months away.
- **Cobalt**: DRC quota cut to 96.6kt/yr — 55% below 2024 exports. >95% Chinese import reliance.
- **Nickel**: Indonesia's quota reduction to 260–270Mt; annual review cycle creates uncertainty.
- **Copper**: Zero treatment charges reflect acute concentrate tightness; Grasberg force majeure; El Teniente tunnel collapse.
- **Graphite**: 98% of graphitization capacity in China — the most concentrated link in the battery supply chain.
- **Chemicals**: China's sulfuric acid export ban + Strait of Hormuz disruption = dual chemical supply shock.

## 5. Technology Watch

### Processing Breakthroughs
- **Valor** (USA) commercialized electrochemical liquid-liquid extraction using "molecular magnets" to separate metals without smelting. First facility in Houston.
- **Idaho National Laboratory** advanced EC-Leach (>95% Li/Co/Ni/Mn recovery with 75% less energy) and biohydrometallurgy using microbes fed on waste.
- **Altilium** (UK) patented EcoMineral™ hydrometallurgical process for copper recovery from tailings, achieving 70% leaching efficiency at Medet.
- **Chinese Academy of Sciences** developed voltage-gated membrane separation for selective extraction from seawater/complex mixtures.
- **Supercritical CO₂ extraction** (Young-Shin Jun, WashU) recovers REEs from coal ash with ~100% purity.

### Substitution
- Analysis at Critical Minerals '26 conference suggests **~70% of copper consumed globally could be replaced by aluminium** in wiring, cabling, and tubing — but entrenched manufacturing preferences and certification barriers remain.
- Sodium-ion batteries (CATL, 40GWh by Q4 2026) offer a cobalt- and lithium-free alternative for stationary storage.

### Recycling
- EU mandates 25% recycled synthetic graphite in batteries by 2030 — currently <3% of demand is met by recycling.
- INL's E-RECOV: room-temperature electrochemical e-waste recycling with 75% fewer chemicals.
- PNNL demonstrated nickel extraction from olivine using waste acid, generating $24/t additional ore value.

## 6. Outlook & Risk Assessment

### Key Risks to Monitor Next Week
1. **Zimbabwe lithium shipments** — If quotas lead to actual exports in the coming weeks, it could ease some supply tightness.
2. **Indonesia supplementary nickel quotas** — Potential Q3 release could cap nickel price upside.
3. **China rare earth controls** — The November 2026 suspension window expiry is a major flashpoint; any escalation could trigger price spikes.
4. **Copper mine restarts** — Whether high prices incentivize restarts at Grasberg and El Teniente.
5. **Iran/Hormuz developments** — Any escalation would deepen the sulfuric acid crisis affecting multiple mineral supply chains.

### Price Forecast Signals
- **Lithium**: Bullish momentum with ¥250,000/t+ expected in 2–3 months, contingent on supply restarts.
- **Cobalt**: Elevated but approaching ceiling; recycling and LFP substitution may cap further upside.
- **Nickel**: Range-bound $18,000–20,000/t near-term; moderate upside only.
- **Copper**: Structural deficit supports $11,000–14,000/t range; tariffs and China slowdown are downside risks.
- **Rare earths**: Heavy REEs remain tight; light REEs adequately supplied from MP/Lynas.

### Geopolitical Flashpoints
- **US-China**: The Cold Resource War is deepening — 73% of mining executives expect greater divergence.
- **Strait of Hormuz**: Chemical supply shock threatens multiple processing value chains.
- **November 2026**: China rare earth control suspension expires — potential for immediate re-escalation.
- **Africa**: Resource nationalism wave could spread from DRC/Zimbabwe to Zambia and South Africa.
