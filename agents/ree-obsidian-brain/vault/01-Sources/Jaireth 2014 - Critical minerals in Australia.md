# Critical minerals in Australia: a review of the mineral potential of REE, Nb, Ta, Li, Co, Bi, Sb, Mo, W and graphite

## Abstract

This comprehensive review assesses Australia's mineral potential for 10 critical commodities including rare earth elements, niobium, tantalum, lithium, cobalt, bismuth, antimony, molybdenum, tungsten, and graphite. The study identifies key deposit types and geological provinces prospective for each commodity, with particular emphasis on REE enrichment in carbonatites, ion-adsorption clays, and heavy mineral sands.

## Key Findings

- Australia hosts significant REE resources in carbonatite complexes (Mount Weld, OLADE), heavy mineral sands (Eneabba, Murray Basin), and ion-adsorption clays in weathered granites
- The Mount Weld deposit (Western Australia) is one of the world's highest-grade REE deposits with >40 Mt at ~15% TREO
- Australian ion-adsorption clay systems in New South Wales and Queensland show HREE-enriched patterns with potential for low-cost extraction
- Known carbonatite complexes include: Mount Weld, OLADE, Gifford Creek, and Mordor Complex
- Heavy mineral sand deposits in the Murray Basin and Eucla Basin contain significant monazite resources
