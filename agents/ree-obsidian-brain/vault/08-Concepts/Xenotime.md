# Xenotime

## Overview
Xenotime is the primary ore mineral for heavy rare earth elements (HREE). Unlike bastnäsite and monazite which are LREE-dominated, xenotime is strongly enriched in Y, Dy, Er, Yb, and other HREE. It is the defining mineral of hydrothermal HREE deposits like Browns Range.

## Properties
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | **YPO₄** |
| Crystal System | Tetragonal (zircon group) |
| Hardness | 4–5 (Mohs) |
| Density | **4.4–5.1 g/cm³** |
| Colour | Yellow-brown, reddish-brown, greyish-green |
| Lustre | Vitreous to resinous |
| Streak | Pale yellow or white |

## REE Content
Xenotime is strongly HREE-enriched:
| Element | Approx. % of REE |
|---|---|
| Y | 50–60% |
| Dy | 8–12% |
| Er | 5–8% |
| Yb | 3–5% |
| Ho | 1–2% |
| Tb | 1–2% |

**LREE (La, Ce)**: <1% combined

## Occurrence
- **Hydrothermal deposits**: Primary ore in HREE-enriched veins (Browns Range)
- **Alkaline complexes**: Accessory in syenites, nepheline syenites
- **Granitic pegmatites**: Large crystals
- **Placer deposits**: Heavy mineral sands (minor)
- **Associated minerals**: Zircon, apatite, allanite, fluorite

## Processing
- **Gravity**: Dense mineral — amenable to gravity separation
- **Fine grind**: Grain size 10–200 μm requires fine crushing
- **Leaching**: HCl or caustic crack
- **Recovery**: 60–75% (lower than bastnäsite due to fine grains)
- **Challenge**: Silica gel formation during acid leach
