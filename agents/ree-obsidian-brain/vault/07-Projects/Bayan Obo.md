# Bayan Obo

## Overview
Bayan Obo ("Rich Hill" in Mongolian) is the world's largest rare earth deposit by contained metal and the cornerstone of Chinese REE dominance. It has produced the majority of global REE for decades and remains the largest single source of LREE. The deposit is also a major iron ore producer, with REE originally extracted as a by-product of iron mining.

## Location
- **Region**: Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China
- **Nearest City**: Baotou (~150 km south)
- **Geology**: Northern margin of North China Craton
- **Elevation**: ~1,400–1,700 m

## Ownership
- **State-owned**: China Northern Rare Earth Group (CNREG)
- **Operator**: Baotou Steel Rare Earth High-Tech Co.
- **Government**: Strategic state-controlled asset

## Geological Debate
Bayan Obo's origin is controversial:
- **Carbonatite model**: Intrusive carbonatite magma
- **Hydrothermal model**: REE-enriched fluids replacing dolomite marble
- **Current consensus**: Carbonatite-derived hydrothermal system

The host rock is **H8 dolomite marble** (Proterozoic), not typical carbonatite. This makes Bayan Obo a unique hybrid deposit type.

## Processing
- **Iron ore first**: Bayan Obo is primarily an iron mine
- **REE from tailings**: REE minerals concentrated from iron beneficiation tailings
- **Flotation**: Bastnäsite + monazite flotation
- **Smelting**: Baotou REE smelting complex (world's largest)
- **Separation**: Solvent extraction at Baotou; produces separated oxides

## Environmental Issues
- **Tailings dam**: World's largest REE tailings pond ("Baotou tailings dam")
- **Groundwater contamination**: Ammonium nitrogen, heavy metals
- **Radioactivity**: Monazite fraction contains Th
- **Reclamation**: Ongoing government-mandated remediation

## Strategic Significance
- **Global dominance**: Largest single REE source (~40% of global production)
- **Supply control**: Chinese government sets production quotas
- **Price setter**: Bayan Obo output directly influences global prices
- **Downstream chain**: Integrated from mine → separation → magnets
