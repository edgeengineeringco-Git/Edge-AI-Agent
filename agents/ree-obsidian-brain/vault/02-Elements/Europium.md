# Europium (Eu)

## Overview
Europium is the most redox-sensitive of all REEs, with a stable +2 state under reducing conditions. The Eu anomaly (Eu/Eu*) is a powerful petrogenetic indicator — it records feldspar fractionation because Eu²⁺ substitutes for Ca²⁺ in plagioclase. Eu is also one of the most valuable REEs due to scarcity and irreplaceable phosphor applications.

## Eu Anomaly Interpretation
| Eu/Eu* | Condition | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.7 | Negative anomaly | Feldspar fractionation (plagioclase removal) |
| 0.7–1.3 | Normal | No significant feldspar effect |
| > 1.3 | Positive anomaly | Plagioclase cumulate or hydrothermal Eu²⁺ enrichment |

## Economic Significance
- **Phosphors**: Red colour in LEDs and displays — **no perfect substitute**
- **Nuclear**: Eu-151/153 neutron cross-sections; control rods
- **Price**: ~$350/kg (35× La baseline, very high volatility)
- **Criticality**: ★★★★★ — functionally irreplaceable

## Exploration Notes
- Eu anomalies are ubiquitous in igneous REE patterns
- Strong positive Eu anomalies may indicate hydrothermal remobilization
- In IAC clays, Eu is often depleted relative to Sm and Gd
