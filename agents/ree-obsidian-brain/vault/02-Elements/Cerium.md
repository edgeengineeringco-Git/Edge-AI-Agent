# Cerium (Ce)

## Overview
Cerium is the most abundant REE in Earth's crust (60 ppm). It is the only REE with a stable +4 oxidation state, making it uniquely useful as an redox proxy in geochemistry. The Ce anomaly (Ce/Ce*) is one of the most powerful exploration indicators for paleo-redox conditions.

## Ce Anomaly Interpretation
| Ce/Ce* | Condition | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| > 1.2 | Positive anomaly | Oxidizing conditions; Ce⁴⁺ immobile |
| 0.8–1.2 | Normal | No significant redox fractionation |
| < 0.8 | Negative anomaly | Reducing conditions or seawater REE pattern |

## Economic Significance
- **Polishing**: CeO₂ is the dominant glass polishing compound
- **Catalysts**: Automotive catalytic converters (Ce-Zr-O solid solutions)
- **Glass**: UV absorption, decolorizing
- **Price**: ~$12/kg (oversupplied, very low volatility)

## Exploration Notes
- Ce anomaly is diagnostic: positive in oxidized profiles, negative in reducing
- In carbonatites, Ce often shows enrichment relative to La and Pr
- Cerianite (CeO₂) is a rare primary mineral but forms during weathering
