# Hydrothermal HREE Deposits

## Overview
Hydrothermal HREE deposits form from magmatic-hydrothermal fluids enriched in F, P, and Ca. They are the primary non-Chinese source of HREE and represent the most promising frontier for Western HREE supply. Key minerals are xenotime (YPO₄) and fluorapatite. The archetype is Browns Range (Northern Minerals, Australia).

## Characteristics
| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| TREO Grade | 0.3–3% |
| HREO:TREO | 30–75% (very HREE-enriched) |
| Key Minerals | Xenotime, fluorapatite, allanite, gadolinite |
| Gangue | Quartz, fluorite, barite, carbonate |
| Alteration | Greisen, sodic, potassic |

## Geochemical Pathfinders
- **Elevated**: F, P, Ca (fluorapatite); Ba, Sr (barite gangue)
- **Depressed**: S (low sulfide)
- **Association**: Granitic to syenitic intrusions

## Major Examples
| Deposit | Country | TREO | HREO:TREO | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Browns Range | Australia | 0.3–1.0% | 55–75% | Developing |
| Nolans Bore | Australia | 2–3% | 3–8% | Advanced |
| Dubbo Zirconia | Australia | 0.5–1% | ~20% | Developing |
| Hoidas Lake | Canada | 0.5–2% | ~40% | Exploration |

## Processing
- **Beneficiation**: Gravity + magnetic + flotation (complex)
- **Leaching**: HCl (eudialyte/fluorapatite) or caustic crack
- **Recovery**: 50–75% (lower than carbonatite)
- **Challenge**: Silica gel formation, complex mineralogy

## Exploration Criteria
1. Fertile peraluminous or alkaline granite source
2. Fault/fracture-controlled fluid pathways
3. Fluorite + barite gangue minerals
4. Elevated Y + HREE in soil geochemistry
5. Low magnetic susceptibility (xenotime is non-magnetic)

## Strengths & Weaknesses
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Very high HREO:TREO | Complex mineralogy |
| Non-Chinese source | Lower recoveries |
| Premium basket price | Higher processing cost |
| Growing demand for HREE | Smaller tonnage than carbonatite |
