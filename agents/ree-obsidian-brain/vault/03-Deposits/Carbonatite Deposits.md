# Carbonatite Deposits

## Overview
Carbonatites are igneous rocks with >50% carbonate minerals, originating from mantle-derived melts. They are the world's primary source of LREE, hosting the largest REE mines: Bayan Obo (China) and Mountain Pass (USA). Carbonatites are also major sources of Nb, P, and sometimes Cu.

## Characteristics
| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| TREO Grade | 3–9% |
| HREO:TREO | 1–5% (LREE-dominant) |
| Key Minerals | Bastnäsite, monazite, huanghoite, apatite |
| Associated Commodities | Nb, Ta, P, Fe, Cu, Sr, Ba |
| Age | Typically Proterozoic to Cenozoic |

## Geochemical Pathfinders
- **Elevated**: Th, Nb, Ba, Sr, P, F, Zr
- **Depressed**: Si, K
- **Radiometric**: Strong Th and U signatures

## Major Examples
| Deposit | Country | TREO | HREO:TREO | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bayan Obo | China | 3–6% | 2–5% | Producing |
| Mountain Pass | USA | 7–9% | 1–3% | Producing |
| Araxá | Brazil | 2–4% | ~15% | Producing |
| Lovozero | Russia | 1–2% | ~20% | Producing |

## Processing
- **Beneficiation**: Flotation (selective) → 50–70% REE concentrate
- **Recovery**: 70–85%
- **Leaching**: H₂SO₄ roast-acid (bastnäsite) or caustic crack (monazite)

## Exploration Criteria
1. Alkaline/ultra-alkaline magmatic association
2. Circular magnetic anomaly (often negative)
3. Elevated Nb + REE in stream sediments
4. Fenite alteration halo (Na-metasomatism)
5. Often associated with kimberlites or lamprophyres

## Strengths & Weaknesses
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Very high grades | Low HREO content |
| Large tonnage | Radioactive Th in monazite varieties |
| Well-understood processing | Acid-intensive processing |
| Established supply chains | Oversupplied LREE market |
