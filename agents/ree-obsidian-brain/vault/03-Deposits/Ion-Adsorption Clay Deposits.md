# Ion-Adsorption Clay (IAC) Deposits

## Overview
Ion-adsorption clay deposits are weathered granite profiles in tropical/subtropical climates where REE ions are adsorbed onto kaolinite and halloysite clay surfaces. They are the world's **dominant source of HREE** and the only deposit type where REE are mined without hard-rock blasting. IAC deposits are overwhelmingly concentrated in southern China (Jiangxi, Guangdong, Fujian).

## Characteristics
| Parameter | Range |
|---|---|
| TREO Grade | 300–3000 ppm (0.03–0.3%) |
| HREO:TREO | 25–45% (HREE-enriched) |
| Key Minerals | None — adsorbed ions on clay surfaces |
| Weathering Depth | 5–30 m |
| CIA Index | >80 (intensely weathered) |

## Geochemical Pathfinders
- **Weathering index**: CIA > 80
- **Low magnetic susceptibility** (granite weathered to clay)
- **Ga, In, Ge** enrichment
- **Granitic source rocks**: Biotite granite, tuff, rhyolite

## Processing
- **Beneficiation**: None — direct leach from ore
- **Leaching**: (NH₄)₂SO₄ or NH₄Cl at 70–100°C
- **Recovery**: 50–80% (in-situ or heap leach)
- **Environmental**: Ammonium leachate management critical

## Exploration Criteria
1. Granitic terrane (S-type or A-type granite)
2. Tropical/subtropical climate (high rainfall, warm)
3. Deep lateritic weathering profile (>10 m)
4. Low topography with good drainage
5. REE-enriched parent rock (primary enrichment)

## Strengths & Weaknesses
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Highest HREO:TREO ratios | Very low grade (ppm, not %) |
| No blasting/mining | Small footprint per deposit |
| Simple leaching | Environmental damage (ammonium) |
| Premium basket price | China dominance (geopolitical risk) |
| Lowest carbon footprint | Resource depletion (near-surface only) |
